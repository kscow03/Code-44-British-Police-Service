Trainer Module 9 — Armed Containment, Searches & High-Risk Warrants

Instructor Lesson Plan

ARU Academy • Cohort 001

Trainer-Only Resource

Lesson Overview

Module 9 introduces students to the principles of armed containment, coordinated searching and high-risk warrant operations within Code44.

Students should demonstrate that they can work within a wider operational plan, understand their assigned role, communicate relevant information, reassess changing circumstances and transition appropriately once the immediate threat has been controlled.

This module should be delivered primarily through briefing exercises, practical containment work and controlled FiveM scenarios.

Lesson Module 9 — Armed Containment, Searches & High-Risk Warrants
Recommended Duration 120–180 minutes
Delivery Style Briefing, containment exercises, searches and scenarios
Assessment Continuous practical observation and knowledge check
Primary Focus Coordination, information, communication and reassessment
Progression Required before Module 10

Mandatory Training Boundary

Module 9 is designed for Code44 FiveM roleplay and server-based training.

Trainers should focus on containment principles, briefing, information handling, communication, role allocation, search discipline, reassessment and post-incident accountability.

Exact real-world entry techniques, room-clearing procedures, breaching mechanics and physical intervention methods are outside the scope of this Academy material.

Building-entry and CQB-specific training is developed further within Module 10.

  • Confirm students completed Module 8

  • Prepare ballistic shield familiarisation where appropriate
  • Prepare main and ARU radio channels

  • Prepare containment scenario injects
  • Prepare changing-threat information

  • Prepare a post-operation handover exercise
  • Prepare Module 9 knowledge check
  • Prepare observation and assessment notes

Learning Outcomes

By the end of Module 9, students should be able to:

  • Explain the purpose of armed containment.
  • Explain how containment can reduce and control operational risk.
  • Identify relevant information before a containment, search or warrant.
  • Recognise information gaps and uncertainty.
  • Understand their own role within a wider operation.
  • Communicate significant observations clearly.
  • Explain the principles of a coordinated armed search.
  • Understand why high-risk warrants require additional planning.
  • Identify when specialist resources may assist.
  • Recognise when the agreed plan is no longer suitable.
  • Respond appropriately to containment failure or subject movement.
  • Complete an accurate post-operation handover.
  • Explain their decisions during debrief.

Opening Script

Suggested wording

“Module 9 is about controlling risk before rushing to resolve it.

Containment, searches and high-risk warrants all require officers to understand the wider plan rather than focusing only on their own immediate task.

Today I will be looking at your information handling, communication, awareness, role discipline and ability to adapt when circumstances change.”

Stage 1 — Purpose of Armed Containment

Explain that containment can provide time and space to manage a developing armed incident.

Containment may support:

  • Limiting subject movement.
  • Reducing risk to members of the public.
  • Maintaining observation where possible.
  • Gathering additional information.
  • Allowing firearms command to develop a plan.
  • Allowing specialist resources to arrive.
  • Supporting communication or negotiation.
  • Preventing unnecessary officers entering the threat area.
Trainer Prompt Why might containment be more appropriate than immediately entering a location?

Expected learning points

  • It may reduce immediate risk.
  • It may allow more information to be gathered.
  • It may allow resources and command to organise.
  • It preserves operational options.
  • Immediate intervention is not always necessary.

Exercise One — Initial Containment

Scenario ARU receives information that a subject linked to a firearms incident has entered a commercial building. No shots are currently being reported. Several civilians may still be inside.

Ask students:

  • What information do we currently have?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Who may be at risk?
  • What could containment achieve?
  • What additional resources may be useful?
  • What information would you communicate immediately?

Expected learning points

  • The subject's presence may still need confirming.
  • Civilians may be at risk.
  • Containment may limit further movement.
  • Information and resources should be developed before unnecessary escalation.
  • The situation must be continually reassessed.

Stage 2 — Information & Threat Assessment

Students should identify:

Subject Identity, description, location and relevant history.
Weapon Reported, suspected or confirmed weapon information.
Occupants Other suspects, civilians, vulnerable persons or hostages.
Environment Location, public presence and relevant environmental information.
Resources ARV, response, NPAS, K9, medical and command resources.
Unknowns Important information that remains unclear.

Exercise Two — Information Classification

Provide students with a mixture of confirmed and unconfirmed intelligence.

Require them to identify:

  • What has been reported.
  • What is known.
  • What they personally observe.
  • What remains suspected.
  • What information may now be outdated.

Challenge unsupported assumptions immediately.

Stage 3 — Roles & Responsibilities

Firearms Command Provides operational oversight and tactical direction where required.
ARV Officers Carry out assigned armed responsibilities within the wider plan.
Containment Officers Maintain assigned observation or control responsibility.
Search Team Carries out the authorised search when directed.
Response May manage cordons, civilians, witnesses and wider scene requirements.
Specialist Resources May include NPAS, K9, medical capability and other authorised support.

Exercise Three — Role Allocation

Provide students with a fictional operational briefing and ask them to explain what each available resource could contribute.

Assess whether they:

  • Understand their own role.
  • Recognise what other officers are responsible for.
  • Avoid attempting to do every task themselves.
  • Understand the overall operational objective.

Stage 4 — Communication During Containment

Relevant updates may include:

  • Subject sightings.
  • Movement.
  • New weapon information.
  • Loss of observation.
  • Civilians entering or leaving the area.
  • Changes in officer positions.
  • Medical emergencies.
  • Changes to the operational plan.
Key teaching point Significant information should be passed promptly, but not every minor observation needs to flood the radio channel.

Scenario One — Containment Lost

During the exercise, the subject is no longer visible and may have moved from the believed location.

Ask:

  • What has changed?
  • What assumptions are now unsafe?
  • Who needs to know?
  • What should happen to the current plan?
  • What additional resources may assist?

Expected learning points

  • The subject's location is now uncertain.
  • The existing containment assessment may no longer be valid.
  • Command and other officers require an update.
  • The plan should be reassessed.
  • NPAS or K9 may assist depending on circumstances.

Stage 5 — Armed Search Principles

Explain that searches should be purposeful and coordinated.

Before the search begins, students should understand:

  • What they are searching for.
  • Why armed officers are required.
  • The believed threat.
  • The assigned search area.
  • The officers involved.
  • Supporting resources.
  • Communication arrangements.
  • What may cause the search to stop or change.
Key teaching point A search is not a race. Students should remain coordinated and communicate relevant changes.

Exercise Four — Controlled Search Scenario

Use a simple Code44 location and provide a clear search objective.

Assess:

  • Shared understanding before the search.
  • Communication.
  • Team awareness.
  • Recognition of civilians or uninvolved persons.
  • Response to changing information.
  • Compliance with trainer direction.
  • Ability to pause when uncertain.

Do not turn this into the full Module 10 CQB lesson.

Stage 6 — High-Risk Warrants

Explain that high-risk warrants require additional planning because the available information suggests an increased level of operational risk.

Possible considerations include:

  • Known or suspected firearms.
  • Violent subjects.
  • Previous threats to police.
  • Relevant intelligence.
  • Other occupants.
  • Public risk.
  • Medical requirements.
  • Specialist resources.
  • Communication arrangements.

Exercise Five — High-Risk Warrant Briefing

Provide students with a fictional warrant package.

Require them to identify:

  • The operational objective.
  • The subject.
  • Weapon information.
  • Other known occupants.
  • Relevant risks.
  • Important unknowns.
  • Available resources.
  • What could cause the plan to change.

Scenario Two — Additional Occupant

During the operation, new information confirms that an additional person is inside who was not included in the original briefing.

Ask:

  • Does this matter?
  • What new risks may exist?
  • Who should be updated?
  • Does the current plan remain suitable?

Expected learning points

  • The new occupant matters.
  • Their identity and involvement may be unknown.
  • Command and participating officers should be informed.
  • The plan may require reassessment.

Scenario Three — Weapon Information Changes

The operation was planned around a reported firearm. New reliable information indicates the weapon is no longer believed to be present.

Ask:

  • What has changed?
  • Does the threat assessment change?
  • Does ARU automatically withdraw?
  • What should happen to the existing plan?

Expected learning points

  • The weapon assessment has materially changed.
  • The wider threat must still be considered.
  • No automatic response applies.
  • The operational plan should be reassessed.

Stage 7 — Specialist Resource Integration

NPAS Observation, tracking and wider situational awareness.
K9 Search, tracking and authorised specialist support.
Ballistic Shield Additional protective capability where appropriate within Code44.
Medical Capability Immediate casualty management where required.
Response Outer scene control, evacuations, witnesses and wider policing.
Firearms Command Tactical oversight and coordination.

Resource Selection

Correct students who treat specialist resources as automatic additions to every operation.

Students should be able to explain what problem a requested resource is intended to solve.

Scenario Four — Public Enters the Containment

During an established containment, an uninvolved person enters the wider operational area.

Ask:

  • What risk has changed?
  • Who may be best placed to manage the person?
  • Does every ARU officer need to abandon their assigned role?
  • What should be communicated?

Expected learning points

  • Public risk has changed.
  • Response or another appropriate resource may manage the issue.
  • Officers should not unnecessarily abandon critical responsibilities.
  • Relevant information should be communicated.

Stage 8 — Resolution & Handover

Once the immediate threat has been controlled, students should consider:

  • Subject status.
  • Outstanding subjects.
  • Weapons or relevant items.
  • Medical requirements.
  • Witnesses.
  • Scene protection.
  • Command updates.
  • Investigator or response handover.
  • ARU equipment accountability.

Exercise Six — Operational Handover

Require each student to provide a concise post-operation handover.

The handover should include:

  • Original information.
  • What the student personally observed.
  • Subject status.
  • Weapons information.
  • Other occupants.
  • Force used if relevant.
  • Medical concerns.
  • Outstanding risks.
  • Further action required.

Stage 9 — Debrief

Ask each student:

  • What information influenced your decisions?
  • What remained uncertain?
  • What was your assigned role?
  • What did you communicate?
  • What changed?
  • How did your plan change?
  • What went well?
  • What would you improve?

Practical Assessment Standard

A satisfactory performance should demonstrate:

  • Understanding of containment objectives.
  • Accurate information handling.
  • Recognition of unknowns.
  • Clear understanding of assigned role.
  • Effective communication.
  • Team awareness.
  • Controlled search behaviour.
  • Recognition of public risk.
  • Appropriate use of specialist resources.
  • Continuous reassessment.
  • Ability to change an unsuitable plan.
  • Clear handover.
  • Honest debrief and accountability.

Review Questions

  1. What is the purpose of armed containment?
  2. What advantages can containment provide?
  3. What information should be considered before a search or warrant?
  4. Why is role allocation important?
  5. What information should be communicated during containment?
  6. Why should an armed search not become a race?
  7. Why might a high-risk warrant require ARU support?
  8. When should specialist resources be requested?
  9. What should happen when circumstances materially change?
  10. What responsibilities remain once the immediate threat is controlled?
  11. What should an officer be able to explain during debrief?

Expected Answers

  1. To control risk, limit subject movement and preserve operational options.
  2. Time, information gathering, resource coordination, safer public management and more controlled decision-making.
  3. Subject, weapon, occupant, location, risk, intelligence, resources, medical considerations and important unknowns.
  4. So officers understand their responsibilities and work toward one coordinated objective.
  5. Subject movement, weapon information, public entry, officer movement, loss of observation, medical issues and significant changes.
  6. Because coordination, awareness and information handling are more important than speed.
  7. Because firearms, violence, intelligence or other risks may require specialist armed capability.
  8. When the resource solves a specific operational problem or supports the tactical objective.
  9. Reassess, communicate and alter, pause or abandon the plan where appropriate.
  10. Medical care, subject accountability, scene management, command updates, evidence or witness considerations, handover and equipment accountability.
  11. Information available, perceived threat, assigned role, observations, decisions, communications and how changing circumstances affected actions.

Instructor Observation Criteria

Containment Understanding Understands the purpose of controlling risk and preserving options.
Information Handling Separates confirmed, reported and unknown information.
Role Discipline Understands and performs their assigned responsibility.
Communication Communicates relevant changes clearly.
Team Awareness Maintains awareness of other officers and wider operations.
Search Discipline Remains controlled and coordinated.
Public Safety Recognises changing risks to uninvolved persons.
Resource Selection Can explain why specialist support is required.
Reassessment Recognises when information or threat changes.
Accountability Explains decisions honestly and coherently.

Immediate Stop Criteria

Stop or reset the exercise if a student:

  • Ignores a trainer safety instruction.
  • Continues despite losing understanding of their role.
  • Acts outside the agreed scenario boundary.
  • Abandons an important assigned responsibility without communication.
  • Repeatedly fails to communicate significant changes.
  • Ignores public or officer risk.
  • Uses specialist resources contrary to trainer direction.
  • Rushes a search despite clear uncertainty.
  • Ignores a serious server or equipment issue.
  • Refuses trainer direction.

Module Outcomes

Pass

The student demonstrates satisfactory containment awareness, communication, search discipline, role understanding, reassessment and accountability.

Pass with Development Points

The student may progress but has limited development areas to continue working on during later Academy scenarios.

Remedial

The student requires additional coaching or controlled scenarios before progressing.

Incomplete

Required pre-learning, practical work or assessment remains outstanding.

Unsuccessful

The student has not met the required standard after appropriate instruction, feedback and reassessment.

Common Student Errors

  • Thinking containment means simply waiting.
  • Ignoring important information gaps.
  • Leaving an assigned responsibility unnecessarily.
  • Overloading the radio with minor information.
  • Failing to report significant movement.
  • Rushing an armed search.
  • Treating every high-risk warrant the same.
  • Requesting specialist resources without explaining why.
  • Failing to adapt when circumstances change.
  • Focusing on the suspect while ignoring civilians or other officers.
  • Giving poor or incomplete handovers.

Common Trainer Errors

  • Making the first containment scenario too complicated.
  • Failing to give students a clear operational objective.
  • Teaching Module 10 CQB content prematurely.
  • Allowing students to rush searches without correction.
  • Failing to introduce changing information.
  • Giving every role to ARU rather than integrating other units.
  • Failing to explain why a specialist resource is being used.
  • Not assessing each student individually.
  • Providing the preferred answer before the debrief.
  • Failing to record development points.

Closing Script

Suggested wording

“That concludes Module 9.

Containment, searches and high-risk warrants rely on patience, coordination and good information.

Being an armed officer does not mean rushing to resolve every incident. Sometimes the best tactical decision is to control the situation, develop the information and preserve safer options.

Module 10 will build on this by moving into building operations, CQB principles and tactical team movement.”
