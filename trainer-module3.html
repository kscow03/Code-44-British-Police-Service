Trainer Module 3 — Legal and Decision-Making

Instructor Lesson Plan

ARU Academy • Cohort 001

Trainer-Only Resource

Lesson Overview

Module 3 introduces students to the legal, policy and accountability principles that support decision-making within Code44 ARU roleplay.

The lesson should focus on reasoning rather than memorising phrases. Students must learn to separate confirmed information from assumptions, consider reasonable options, explain their decisions and reassess when circumstances change.

Lesson Module 3 — Legal and Decision-Making
Recommended Duration 90 minutes
Current Group Two ARU students
Delivery Style Discussion, tabletop exercises and written justification
Assessment Observation, written task and knowledge check
Pass Standard Satisfactory rationale and 80% quiz score

This lesson is written for Code44 roleplay. Trainers must deliver it alongside current server rules, approved ARU policy and command direction.

Where current policy differs from this lesson plan, current approved policy takes priority.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, each student should be able to:

  • Explain why operational decisions must be justified.
  • Describe necessity, proportionality and reasonableness.
  • Separate confirmed information from assumptions.
  • Identify risks to officers, subjects and members of the public.
  • Consider more than one reasonable option.
  • Provide a clear APSTRA update.
  • Explain why decisions must be reassessed.
  • Recognise when command should be updated.
  • Describe post-incident priorities.
  • Produce a clear written rationale.

Opening Script

Suggested wording

“Module 3 focuses on legal and accountable decision-making.

As an ARU officer, it is not enough to say that you believed an action was right. You must be able to explain what information you had, what risk you identified, what options you considered and why your decision was necessary and proportionate.

This lesson is not about memorising one perfect answer. It is about showing clear, defensible and flexible reasoning.”

Stage 1 — Responsibility and Accountability

Explain that armed policing carries increased responsibility because decisions may have serious consequences.

An ARU officer should be prepared to explain:

  • What information was available.
  • What threat was identified.
  • Who was at risk.
  • What options were considered.
  • Why the chosen option was necessary.
  • Why the response was proportionate.
  • How circumstances changed.
  • What happened afterwards.
Trainer prompt Why is “I thought it was the right thing to do” not enough on its own?

Expected learning point

The officer must explain the facts, risk, options and reasoning that supported the decision.

Stage 2 — Core Decision-Making Principles

Protection of Life Preservation of life remains the primary objective.
Necessity The action must genuinely be required.
Proportionality The response must be balanced against the threat.
Reasonableness The decision should make sense based on what was honestly known.
Accountability The officer must be able to explain and record the decision.
Reassessment The decision must change when the circumstances change.

Discussion — Necessary or Merely Available?

Ask both students:

Question Does having an authorised tactic or force option available automatically mean it should be used?

Expected learning points

  • Availability does not equal necessity.
  • The option must fit the actual threat.
  • Reasonable alternatives should be considered.
  • The officer must explain why the chosen response was appropriate.
  • The response must be reviewed as circumstances change.

Stage 3 — Information Before Action

Explain that officers must distinguish between different levels of information.

Reported Information received from a caller, witness or other source.
Observed Information personally seen or heard by an officer.
Confirmed Information supported by reliable evidence or direct verification.
Unknown Important information that has not yet been established.

Relevant information may include:

  • Caller reliability
  • Witness accounts
  • Officer observations
  • CCTV or other evidence
  • Subject description
  • Subject behaviour
  • Presence of members of the public
  • Presence of vulnerable persons
  • Available support
  • Whether the situation is changing

Scenario One — Unconfirmed Firearm Report

ARU is deployed to reports of a male carrying a handgun near a busy public area. The caller has left. Officers locate a male matching the description, but no weapon is visible.

Ask each student:

  • What information is reported?
  • What information is confirmed?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Who may be at risk?
  • What reasonable options could be considered?
  • What should an APSTRA update include?

Expected learning points

  • The initial report remains relevant but is not proof.
  • The matching description supports further assessment.
  • The public location increases potential risk.
  • Officers should avoid treating assumptions as confirmed fact.
  • Communication, containment and further observation may be considered.
  • The plan should remain flexible.

Stage 4 — Five-Step Decision-Making Mindset

  1. Identify the threat.
    What is happening, who is at risk and how immediate is the danger?
  2. Gather and assess information.
    What is reported, observed, confirmed or unknown?
  3. Consider available options.
    Consider communication, containment, time, distance and support.
  4. Apply necessity and proportionality.
    Is the selected option required and balanced against the threat?
  5. Communicate, record and review.
    Give an APSTRA update and reassess as circumstances change.

Stage 5 — APSTRA

Explain that APSTRA provides a concise structure for communicating the officer's understanding and intended action.

Who Officer, rank, unit or callsign.
What Incident, threat or behaviour.
Where Clear location.
Risk Risk to officers, public, subject or vulnerable persons.
Action Intended action and concise justification.
Example “PC Bloggs, ARV-2. Reports of a firearm near Legion Square. One male matching the description, weapon not yet confirmed, high public presence. Establishing containment and requesting additional units.”

Emphasise that APSTRA should be:

  • Concise
  • Relevant
  • Accurate
  • Updated when circumstances change
  • Clear about what is confirmed and unconfirmed

APSTRA Practice

Give each student one minute to prepare an APSTRA update for Scenario One.

Ask one student to deliver the update, then ask the second student to identify what was clear and what could be improved.

Assess for:

  • Clear officer or unit identification
  • Accurate incident summary
  • Clear location
  • Identified risk
  • Proportionate intended action
  • No unsupported assumptions

Stage 6 — Reassessment

Explain that a decision may be reasonable when made but may become inappropriate if circumstances change.

Officers should continually reassess:

  • Whether the subject still matches the information.
  • Whether a weapon remains visible or confirmed.
  • Whether the threat remains immediate.
  • Whether the subject is complying.
  • Whether the public is entering the area.
  • Whether the chosen action remains necessary.
  • Whether command or additional resources are required.

Scenario Two — Subject Complies

Officers challenge a subject believed to be armed. The subject immediately follows directions, keeps their hands visible and moves away from the suspected weapon.

Ask:

  • How has the threat changed?
  • What actions remain necessary?
  • What force may no longer be justified?
  • What control measures may still be required?
  • What should be communicated to command?

Expected learning points

  • Compliance may reduce the immediacy of the threat.
  • Officers must continue controlling the incident safely.
  • Force should reduce when the justification reduces.
  • The suspected weapon may still require control and recovery.
  • APSTRA and command updates should reflect the change.

Scenario Three — Conflicting Information

After containment is established, a second witness states that the suspected firearm may be a realistic imitation being used during filming.

Ask:

  • Does the new information remove all risk?
  • How should the original plan be reviewed?
  • What further enquiries may be useful?
  • How should the uncertainty be communicated?
  • What would justify escalation or de-escalation?

Expected learning points

  • New information must be considered rather than ignored.
  • The imitation report does not automatically prove the item is safe.
  • Officers should avoid unnecessary escalation.
  • Further observation and verification may be appropriate.
  • The APSTRA update should reflect the conflicting information.

Stage 7 — Command and Escalation

Command updates should include:

  • Current threat
  • Location
  • Subjects and persons at risk
  • Actions already taken
  • Resources present
  • Resources required
  • Any force used
  • Any casualties or urgent medical needs

Explain that officers should not delay immediate action required to protect life solely because a commander is not yet present.

Command should be updated as soon as reasonably possible.

Stage 8 — Aftercare and Reporting

Post-incident priorities may include:

  • Confirming the immediate threat has ended.
  • Providing or requesting urgent medical assistance.
  • Protecting officers, subjects and the public.
  • Updating Force Control and firearms command.
  • Preserving the scene where possible.
  • Identifying witnesses.
  • Completing required reports.
  • Participating in debrief and welfare processes.
Core principle Life-saving treatment takes priority over evidence preservation.

A clear written account should cover:

  • Information initially received
  • Personal observations
  • Threat and risk identified
  • Options considered
  • Action taken and why
  • Changes in circumstances
  • Any force used
  • Aftercare and notifications

Written Decision Exercise

Ask each student to select one of the three scenarios and write a short decision rationale.

The response must include:

  • Known information
  • Unconfirmed information
  • Threat and risk
  • Options considered
  • Chosen action
  • Why the action is necessary
  • Why the action is proportionate
  • What would cause the decision to change

Written Exercise Assessment Standard

A satisfactory response should demonstrate:

  • Clear separation between fact and assumption.
  • Recognition of risk to everyone involved.
  • Consideration of more than one option.
  • A proportionate and defensible decision.
  • Recognition that circumstances may change.
  • Clear communication and accountability.

A student who provides only a conclusion without explaining their reasoning should receive further questioning or remedial learning.

Review Questions

  1. What is the difference between reported and confirmed information?
  2. What does necessity mean?
  3. What does proportionality mean?
  4. Why must decisions be reassessed?
  5. What should an APSTRA update contain?
  6. Why does access to an option not automatically justify its use?
  7. When should command be updated?
  8. Why should force reduce when the threat reduces?
  9. What are the main post-incident priorities?
  10. What should a written rationale explain?

Expected Answers

  1. Reported information comes from another source. Confirmed information has been reliably verified.
  2. The action must genuinely be required.
  3. The response must be balanced against the threat and intended objective.
  4. Circumstances and information can change.
  5. Who, what, where, risk and intended action with justification.
  6. Availability does not prove necessity or proportionality.
  7. When material information changes, further support is required, force is used or important developments occur.
  8. The justification for force weakens when the threat weakens.
  9. Safety, medical care, command updates, scene preservation, witnesses, reports and welfare.
  10. Information, observations, risk, options, chosen action, justification, changes and aftercare.

Instructor Observation Criteria

Information Assessment Separates fact, report and assumption.
Risk Recognition Identifies who may be at risk.
Options Considers more than one response.
Proportionality Selects a balanced response.
APSTRA Communicates clearly and concisely.
Reassessment Changes decisions when circumstances change.
Accountability Explains and records reasoning.
Professionalism Participates calmly and respectfully.

Module Outcomes

Pass

Student demonstrates satisfactory decision-making, APSTRA and written justification.

Pass with Development Points

Student may progress but must address identified areas of reasoning, communication or reassessment.

Remedial

Student requires further teaching, scenario discussion or reassessment.

Incomplete

Required attendance, written work or assessment remains outstanding.

Unsuccessful

Student has not demonstrated the required standard after appropriate support.

Common Student Errors

  • Treating a report as confirmed fact.
  • Failing to identify who is at risk.
  • Selecting an action before considering alternatives.
  • Believing deployment automatically justifies force.
  • Giving vague APSTRA updates.
  • Failing to reassess when circumstances change.
  • Continuing force after the threat reduces.
  • Writing conclusions without explaining reasoning.

Common Trainer Errors

  • Demanding one fixed tactical answer.
  • Focusing on the conclusion rather than the reasoning.
  • Providing the expected answer too early.
  • Allowing one student to dominate.
  • Failing to challenge unsupported assumptions.
  • Ignoring weak APSTRA communication.
  • Failing to record development points.
  • Using outdated policy examples.

Closing Script

Suggested wording

“That concludes Module 3.

Good decision-making is not simply choosing an action. It is understanding the information, recognising the risks, considering options and explaining why the chosen response was necessary and proportionate.

These principles will continue to be assessed throughout every practical Academy exercise.”
