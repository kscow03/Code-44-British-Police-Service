Module 2 — Professional Standards

Code44 Police Service

Armed Response Unit Academy

Cohort 001

Module Overview

Professional standards form the foundation of service within the Armed Response Unit. Technical ability alone does not make a reliable ARU officer.

Throughout the Academy, students are expected to demonstrate integrity, accountability, respect, teamwork and a genuine willingness to develop.

These standards apply during lessons, practical training, operational roleplay, Discord communication and interaction with the wider police service.

1. Learning Objectives

After completing Module 2, you should be able to:

  • Explain what professionalism means within ARU.
  • Describe the importance of honesty and integrity.
  • Understand personal accountability for actions and decisions.
  • Communicate respectfully and effectively.
  • Explain the importance of teamwork and trust.
  • Receive and apply constructive feedback.
  • Understand attendance and preparation expectations.
  • Recognise the importance of confidentiality and discretion.
  • Raise concerns through appropriate professional channels.

2. Professionalism

Professionalism is the consistent standard of behaviour expected from every ARU student and qualified officer.

It is not limited to operational incidents. Your conduct during Academy lessons, Discord discussions, briefings and routine interaction also reflects upon the unit.

A professional ARU officer:

  • Treats colleagues and members of the public respectfully.
  • Communicates calmly and clearly.
  • Arrives prepared for training and deployments.
  • Follows lawful and reasonable instructions.
  • Represents the unit positively.
  • Maintains composure during difficult situations.
  • Separates personal disagreements from professional duties.
  • Recognises the effect their behaviour has on others.
Consider: Can an officer be technically capable but still be unsuitable for ARU because of their attitude, conduct or treatment of others?

3. Integrity

Integrity means being honest, trustworthy and willing to do the right thing even when doing so may be uncomfortable.

Examples of integrity include:

  • Admitting mistakes promptly.
  • Providing accurate accounts of incidents.
  • Not exaggerating qualifications or experience.
  • Completing assessments independently.
  • Not hiding errors made by yourself or another officer.
  • Using internal access appropriately.
  • Being honest about attendance and availability.

Mistakes during training are expected. Dishonesty about a mistake is normally more serious than the original error because it damages trust.

4. Accountability

Accountability means accepting responsibility for your actions, decisions, attendance, equipment and professional conduct.

Students are accountable for:

  • Following Academy instructions and standards.
  • Completing required learning and assessments.
  • Reporting problems or limitations promptly.
  • Explaining and reflecting upon decisions.
  • Applying feedback during later training.
  • Maintaining any equipment entrusted to them.
  • Arranging catch-up learning after missed sessions.
Example: If you miss a scheduled lesson, you should contact a trainer, explain the circumstances and arrange to complete the missed learning.

5. Professional Communication

Effective communication helps prevent confusion, builds trust and supports coordinated teamwork.

Professional communication includes:

  • Listening fully before responding.
  • Using clear and concise language.
  • Addressing colleagues and supervisors appropriately.
  • Asking questions when instructions are unclear.
  • Avoiding unnecessary arguments during briefings.
  • Giving relevant information without dominating communication.
  • Remaining respectful during disagreement.
  • Using the correct Discord or radio channel.

Confidence is encouraged, but confidence must not become arrogance. Students should contribute while recognising the experience and responsibilities of others.

6. Teamwork and Trust

ARU officers work as part of a team. Individual ability is valuable, but reliability, communication and trust are essential.

Effective team members:

  • Share relevant information.
  • Support colleagues without always being asked.
  • Carry out assigned responsibilities.
  • Ask for assistance when required.
  • Recognise the strengths and limitations of others.
  • Resolve disagreements professionally.
  • Place team performance above personal recognition.
  • Accept that other officers may have different perspectives.
Key Principle: Academy training is not a competition between students. The objective is to develop a reliable team capable of working to a shared standard.

7. Receiving and Applying Feedback

Constructive feedback is a core part of Academy development. It is intended to improve performance and should not automatically be treated as personal criticism.

When receiving feedback:

  1. Listen without immediately becoming defensive.
  2. Ask questions if the feedback is unclear.
  3. Reflect upon the issue identified.
  4. Agree an improvement action where appropriate.
  5. Apply the feedback during future training.
  6. Request additional support if required.

You may respectfully disagree or ask for clarification, but you should remain professional and focus on the issue rather than the person.

8. Attendance and Commitment

Cohort 001 requires regular attendance and meaningful participation. Trainers invest time preparing and delivering each lesson, and students should demonstrate an equivalent level of commitment.

Students are expected to:

  • Attend scheduled sessions wherever reasonably possible.
  • Notify trainers in advance when unavailable.
  • Arrive on time and prepared.
  • Complete assigned work before agreed deadlines.
  • Engage appropriately during discussions and exercises.
  • Arrange catch-up learning when sessions are missed.
  • Raise difficulties before they become repeated problems.

Repeated unexplained absence, poor engagement or failure to complete required work may delay progression or lead to removal from the Academy.

9. Confidentiality and Discretion

Access to the Internal Database and Academy resources is provided for authorised Code44 ARU purposes.

Students must not share:

  • Restricted training documents.
  • Assessment questions or answers.
  • Trainer-only resources.
  • Personal student records.
  • Internal command discussions.
  • Password or access information.
  • Screenshots from restricted areas.

Students must not attempt to bypass access restrictions or enter the Trainers Area without authorisation.

10. Rank, Respect and Professional Challenge

Rank and command responsibilities must be respected. Supervisors and trainers are responsible for maintaining standards, directing lessons and making progression decisions.

Respect for rank does not mean remaining silent when something is unclear or concerning. Professional challenge is acceptable when raised properly.

A professional challenge should be:

  • Calm and respectful.
  • Based upon a genuine concern.
  • Raised at an appropriate time where possible.
  • Focused on the issue rather than the person.
  • Escalated through the correct channel if unresolved.
Professional challenge: “Sergeant, could I clarify the instruction? My understanding was that we were waiting for further information before continuing.”

11. Discord and Internal-System Conduct

Professional standards apply to Discord channels, direct messages, website access, Google Forms and other internal systems.

Students should:

  • Use the correct channel for questions and updates.
  • Avoid repeatedly tagging command staff unnecessarily.
  • Keep internal discussions professional.
  • Complete forms and records honestly.
  • Avoid sharing restricted screenshots.
  • Respect decisions made through the command structure.
  • Avoid public arguments about Academy decisions.
  • Raise concerns through the proper internal route.

ARU Professional Values

Professionalism Maintain a consistent and credible standard.
Integrity Be honest, fair and trustworthy.
Accountability Take responsibility for actions and decisions.
Respect Treat others fairly and professionally.
Teamwork Contribute to collective success.
Development Learn continuously and apply feedback.

12. Revision Questions

Consider these questions before attending the trainer-led Module 2 lesson:

  1. What does professionalism look like within ARU?
  2. Why can dishonesty be more serious than the original mistake?
  3. What does personal accountability mean during the Academy?
  4. How should you respond to constructive feedback?
  5. What should you do if you cannot attend a scheduled lesson?
  6. Why is teamwork more important than individual recognition?
  7. How can a disagreement be raised professionally?
  8. What information must not be shared outside authorised channels?
  9. Does strong technical performance excuse poor conduct?
  10. How can your behaviour affect trust in the wider unit?
