Module 2 — Professional Standards

Code44 Armed Response Unit Academy

Cohort 001

Professional Standards Module

Module Overview

Professional standards form the foundation of service within the Armed Response Unit. Technical ability alone does not make an effective ARU officer. Recruits must demonstrate integrity, accountability, respect, discipline and a willingness to develop.

These standards apply throughout the Academy and continue after qualification. A recruit's conduct, attitude and response to feedback will be assessed during every training session.

Estimated Duration 60–75 minutes
Delivery Method Classroom discussion and group activities
Assessment Knowledge check and instructor observation
Pass Standard 80% knowledge-check score
Progression Requirement Required before practical development

Learning Objectives

By completing this module, recruits will be able to:

  • Explain what professionalism means within the Armed Response Unit.
  • Describe the importance of integrity and honesty.
  • Understand personal accountability for actions and decisions.
  • Demonstrate respectful and effective communication.
  • Explain the importance of teamwork and collective responsibility.
  • Receive and apply constructive feedback appropriately.
  • Understand attendance, preparation and commitment expectations.
  • Recognise the importance of confidentiality and discretion.

1. Professionalism

Professionalism is the consistent standard of behaviour expected from every ARU recruit and qualified officer. It applies during operational roleplay, training, briefings, Discord communication and interaction with the wider police service.

A professional ARU officer:

  • Treats colleagues and members of the public respectfully.
  • Communicates calmly and clearly.
  • Arrives prepared for training and deployments.
  • Follows lawful and reasonable instructions.
  • Represents the unit positively.
  • Maintains composure during difficult situations.
  • Separates personal disagreements from professional responsibilities.
Discussion Point: Can an officer be technically capable but still unsuitable for ARU because of their attitude or conduct?

2. Integrity

Integrity means being honest, trustworthy and willing to do the right thing even when doing so may be uncomfortable.

Examples of integrity include:

  • Admitting mistakes promptly.
  • Providing accurate accounts of incidents.
  • Not exaggerating qualifications or experience.
  • Completing assessments independently.
  • Not concealing errors made by yourself or others.
  • Using internal access appropriately.

Mistakes during training are expected. Dishonesty about a mistake is more serious than the original error because it damages trust.

3. Accountability

Accountability means accepting responsibility for your actions, decisions, attendance, equipment and professional conduct.

Recruits are accountable for:

  • Following Academy instructions and standards.
  • Completing required learning and assessments.
  • Maintaining issued equipment appropriately.
  • Reporting problems or limitations promptly.
  • Explaining and reflecting upon decisions.
  • Taking reasonable steps to improve following feedback.
Example: If a recruit misses a training session, they should contact an instructor, explain the circumstances and arrange to complete any missed learning.

4. Communication

Effective communication helps prevent confusion, builds trust and supports safe, coordinated teamwork.

Professional communication includes:

  • Listening fully before responding.
  • Using clear and concise language.
  • Addressing senior officers appropriately.
  • Asking questions when instructions are unclear.
  • Avoiding unnecessary arguments during briefings.
  • Giving relevant information without dominating communication.
  • Remaining respectful during disagreement.

Confidence is encouraged, but confidence must not become arrogance. Recruits should be willing to contribute while recognising the experience and responsibilities of others.

5. Teamwork

ARU officers work as part of a team. Individual ability is valuable, but team reliability, communication and trust are essential.

Effective team members:

  • Share relevant information.
  • Support colleagues without being asked.
  • Carry out assigned responsibilities.
  • Ask for assistance when necessary.
  • Recognise the strengths and limitations of others.
  • Resolve disagreements professionally.
  • Place team performance above personal recognition.
Key Principle: ARU training is not a competition between recruits. The objective is to develop a reliable team capable of working to a shared standard.

6. Receiving Feedback

Constructive feedback is a core part of Academy development. Feedback is intended to improve performance and should not automatically be treated as personal criticism.

When receiving feedback, recruits should:

  1. Listen without immediately becoming defensive.
  2. Ask questions if the feedback is unclear.
  3. Reflect on the issue identified.
  4. Agree an improvement action where appropriate.
  5. Apply the feedback during future training.
  6. Request further support if needed.

Instructors are also expected to give feedback fairly, respectfully and with clear examples.

7. Attendance and Commitment

Cohort 001 requires regular attendance and meaningful participation. Instructors invest time preparing and delivering each lesson, and recruits are expected to show an equivalent level of commitment.

Recruits are expected to:

  • Attend scheduled sessions wherever reasonably possible.
  • Notify instructors in advance when unavailable.
  • Arrive on time and prepared.
  • Complete assigned work before deadlines.
  • Engage appropriately during discussions and exercises.
  • Arrange catch-up learning when sessions are missed.

Repeated unexplained absence, poor engagement or failure to complete required work may result in remedial action or removal from the Academy.

8. Confidentiality and Discretion

Access to the Internal Database and Academy resources is provided for authorised Code44 ARU purposes.

Recruits must not share:

  • Restricted training documents.
  • Assessment questions or answers.
  • Trainer-only resources.
  • Personal recruit records.
  • Internal command discussions.
  • Password or access information.

Officers should use discretion when discussing unit matters and should raise concerns through appropriate internal channels.

9. Rank, Respect and Professional Challenge

Rank and command responsibilities must be respected. Recruits should follow lawful instructions and understand that supervisors are responsible for maintaining standards, coordinating training and making progression decisions.

Respect for rank does not mean remaining silent when something is unclear or concerning. Professional challenge is encouraged when it is raised appropriately.

A professional challenge should be:

  • Calm and respectful.
  • Based on a genuine concern.
  • Raised at an appropriate time where possible.
  • Focused on the issue rather than the person.
  • Escalated through the correct channel if unresolved.

10. Conduct Within Discord and Internal Systems

Professional standards also apply to Discord channels, direct messages, website access and internal forms.

Recruits should:

  • Use the correct channels for questions and updates.
  • Avoid unnecessary tagging of command staff.
  • Keep internal discussions professional.
  • Use forms and records honestly.
  • Avoid sharing screenshots from restricted areas.
  • Respect decisions made through the chain of command.

ARU Professional Values

Professionalism Maintain a consistent and credible standard.
Integrity Be honest, fair and trustworthy.
Accountability Take responsibility for actions and decisions.
Respect Treat others fairly and professionally.
Teamwork Contribute to collective success.
Development Learn continuously and apply feedback.

Group Activity — Defining Professionalism

Recruits should work in small groups and answer the following question:

What does a professional Code44 ARU officer look like in practice?

Each group should identify:

  • Three behaviours expected from an ARU officer.
  • Three behaviours that would damage trust in the unit.
  • One example of professional disagreement.
  • One example of accountability following a mistake.

Groups should present their answers to the instructor for discussion.

Scenario Discussion One

A recruit repeatedly interrupts an instructor during a briefing and dismisses feedback from other members of the cohort.

Discuss:

  • What standards are not being met?
  • How should the issue initially be addressed?
  • What should happen if the behaviour continues?
  • How might this behaviour affect the wider cohort?

Scenario Discussion Two

A recruit makes an error during an assessment but immediately reports it, explains what happened and asks how to improve.

Discuss:

  • What positive standards have been demonstrated?
  • Why is honesty important in this situation?
  • How should an instructor respond?
  • Should the recruit automatically fail because an error occurred?

Scenario Discussion Three

Two recruits disagree during a group exercise. One recruit begins criticising the other in front of the wider cohort and refuses to continue working with them.

Discuss:

  • How should the disagreement be managed?
  • What would a professional response look like?
  • When should an instructor become involved?
  • How can trust be rebuilt afterwards?

Individual Reflection

Each recruit should provide a short written response to the following:

What personal standard will you hold yourself to throughout Cohort 001, and how will you demonstrate it?

The response should include one practical commitment, such as arriving prepared, asking for feedback, supporting another recruit or completing required work on time.

This response is not graded. It should be retained and revisited during the recruit's final Academy interview.

Important Standard

Technical performance does not excuse poor conduct. A recruit may meet practical standards but still be considered unsuitable if they repeatedly demonstrate dishonesty, poor attitude, disrespect or an unwillingness to develop.

Recruit Completion Checklist

  • Attend the Module 2 classroom session
  • Review all professional values
  • Participate in the group activity
  • Participate in the scenario discussions
  • Complete the individual reflection task
  • Complete the Module 2 knowledge check
  • Receive instructor confirmation

Module Completion

Module 2 is complete once the recruit has attended the lesson, participated appropriately, completed the reflection task and achieved the required score in the knowledge check.

Professional conduct remains continuously assessed throughout every subsequent Academy module.
