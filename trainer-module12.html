Trainer Module 12 — Consolidation & Scenario Development

Instructor Development & Final Preparation Pack

ARU Academy • Cohort 001

Trainer-Only Resource

Module Overview

Module 12 is the final development stage before the ARU Academy Final Assessment.

The purpose of this module is not to introduce significant new learning. Trainers should instead consolidate the standards developed throughout Modules 1–11, identify any remaining weaknesses and provide students with a final opportunity to apply feedback before formal assessment.

Module 12 should therefore be highly individualised. Two students may require completely different exercises depending on their previous performance.

Lesson Module 12 — Consolidation & Scenario Development
Recommended Duration Flexible — based on student development needs
Delivery Style Scenario training, coaching, remedial work and debrief
Assessment Developmental readiness assessment
Primary Focus Consistency and final development
Progression Recommendation for Module 13

Module 12 Philosophy

Module 12 should not be treated as a hidden final assessment.

The objective is to identify weaknesses while trainers can still coach, explain and allow the student to demonstrate improvement.

A student who makes a mistake, understands the feedback and corrects it during a later scenario may demonstrate stronger development than a student who simply completes an easy scenario without challenge.

Trainer Preparation

  • Review the student's Module 1–11 training records
  • Review previous knowledge-check results
  • Review recorded development points
  • Identify any repeated development issues
  • Identify areas of strong performance
  • Prepare at least one general consolidation scenario
  • Prepare one student-specific development scenario
  • Prepare an information-handling challenge
  • Prepare a communication challenge
  • Prepare a reassessment challenge
  • Prepare a teamwork or role-discipline exercise
  • Prepare changing information or threat injects
  • Prepare an operational handover exercise
  • Prepare final readiness notes
  • Confirm Module 13 requirements are understood

Review Previous Performance

Before training begins, trainers should review the student's Academy history.

Consider:

Professionalism Has conduct remained consistently professional?
Knowledge Have previous knowledge checks identified recurring weaknesses?
Communication Is communication clear, relevant and timely?
Teamwork Does the student support the team or attempt to dominate?
Decision-Making Are decisions reasonable and explainable?
Reassessment Does the student recognise when circumstances change?
Safety Are firearms, vehicle and equipment standards reliable?
Feedback Has previous trainer feedback actually been applied?

Suggested Delivery Structure

Stage Trainer Action
Student Review Review previous strengths, development points and current training needs.
Student Self-Assessment Ask the student what they believe their strongest and weakest areas are.
Consolidation Scenario Run a scenario requiring several previously learned skills.
Debrief Identify strengths and specific development points.
Targeted Development Provide coaching or a smaller exercise addressing the identified weakness.
Reassessment Scenario Test whether the student applies the feedback without excessive prompting.
Final Review Decide whether the student is ready for Module 13 or requires further development.

Opening Script

Suggested wording

“Module 12 is your final development module before assessment.

This is not another classroom lesson and it is not designed to catch you out.

We are going to look at what you have learned throughout the Academy, identify anything that still needs work and give you an opportunity to improve it before Module 13.

Be honest about what you are confident with and what you think you still need to work on.”

Exercise One — Student Self-Assessment

Ask the student:

  • What do you think your strongest area is?
  • What do you think your weakest area is?
  • Which module did you find most difficult?
  • Which practical element do you feel least confident with?
  • What feedback have trainers repeatedly given you?
  • What have you done differently because of that feedback?
  • What would you like another opportunity to practise?

Compare their self-assessment with previous trainer records.

Academy-Wide Standards

By Module 12, trainers should expect students to demonstrate the following without constant prompting:

Professionalism Professional and accountable conduct.
Integrity Honest reporting of mistakes and uncertainty.
Information Handling Separates confirmed, reported and unknown information.
Communication Passes relevant information clearly.
Teamwork Contributes effectively without unnecessarily dominating.
Decision-Making Makes reasonable decisions based on current circumstances.
Reassessment Recognises when the plan needs to change.
Safety Maintains reliable firearms, equipment and vehicle standards.
Awareness Remains aware of colleagues, subjects and civilians.
Accountability Can explain decisions after an exercise.

Exercise Two — General Consolidation Scenario

Run a scenario that incorporates several previous Academy learning areas.

Possible components include:

  • Initial ARV deployment information.
  • A reported firearm.
  • A vehicle element.
  • Containment or searching.
  • A building element.
  • Multiple persons.
  • A civilian.
  • A casualty.
  • A supporting resource.
  • A material change in information.

Keep the scenario realistic enough that the student can demonstrate judgement rather than simply react to endless trainer injects.

Consolidation Scenario Assessment

Preparation Understands the initial briefing and asks relevant questions.
Information Handles confirmed, reported and unknown information correctly.
Communication Communicates significant information appropriately.
Teamwork Works effectively with their crew and wider resources.
Decision-Making Makes reasonable and proportionate decisions.
Reassessment Adjusts when relevant circumstances change.
Safety Maintains previous safety standards.
Awareness Avoids tunnel vision and considers the wider incident.
Handover Provides a clear and accurate summary afterwards.
Accountability Can explain why actions were taken.

Identifying Development Points

Trainers should identify specific behaviours rather than vague criticism.

Weak Feedback “Your comms weren't very good.”
Better Feedback “You identified that the subject had moved but did not communicate it, which meant the other crew continued operating on outdated information.”

Development points should explain:

  • What happened.
  • Why it mattered.
  • What standard was expected.
  • What the student should do differently next time.

Targeted Development Training

Do not rerun an entire complex scenario if the student only needs development in one small area.

Examples:

Weak Radio Communication Run short information-relay and APSTRA-style communication exercises.
Poor Reassessment Run short scenarios where one important fact changes.
Tunnel Vision Introduce civilians, additional officers or changing priorities.
Poor Teamwork Run an exercise where both students require information held by the other.
Information Assumptions Give deliberately incomplete or conflicting intelligence.
Poor Handover Repeat short handover exercises until the structure improves.

Exercise Three — Reassessment Challenge

Give the student a simple scenario with an obvious initial plan.

Then change one significant fact.

Possible changes:

  • The reported firearm is disproved.
  • A firearm is newly confirmed.
  • The subject becomes compliant.
  • The subject moves location.
  • A civilian enters the scenario.
  • A casualty occurs.
  • A second subject is identified.
  • The target vehicle is no longer confirmed.

Assess whether the student:

  • Notices the change.
  • Communicates it.
  • Updates their assessment.
  • Changes the plan where necessary.

Exercise Four — Information Handling Challenge

Provide a briefing containing:

  • Confirmed information.
  • Reported information.
  • Outdated information.
  • One assumption made by another officer.
  • Important missing information.

Ask the student to explain what they currently understand about the incident.

Assess whether they create certainty where none exists.

Exercise Five — Communication Challenge

During a scenario, introduce several changes.

Some should require communication and some should not.

Assess whether the student can distinguish between:

  • Information the crew needs immediately.
  • Information command needs.
  • Information that can wait.
  • Information that is not operationally relevant.

Exercise Six — Teamwork Challenge

Give each student different but complementary information.

Neither student should have enough information to fully understand the scenario without communicating with the other.

Assess:

  • Information sharing.
  • Listening.
  • Respectful challenge.
  • Shared decision-making.
  • Whether one student unnecessarily dominates.

Do Not Create Artificial Failure

Module 12 scenarios should be challenging but fair.

Avoid:

  • Providing impossible amounts of information.
  • Changing every fact simultaneously.
  • Creating hidden rules the student could not reasonably know.
  • Penalising students for server problems.
  • Expecting one specific solution where several reasonable options exist.
  • Using scenarios purely to embarrass or catch students out.

Trainer Debrief Structure

Allow the student to speak before giving the trainer assessment.

Ask:

  1. What did you understand the incident to be?
  2. What was your immediate priority?
  3. What went well?
  4. What did you find difficult?
  5. What information influenced your decisions?
  6. What changed during the scenario?
  7. Did your plan change?
  8. What would you do differently?

Then provide trainer feedback.

Applying Feedback

Where practical, students should be given another opportunity to demonstrate improvement after feedback.

The reassessment does not need to repeat the exact same scenario.

In fact, changing the circumstances slightly is useful because it tests whether the student understood the underlying principle rather than memorised the answer.

Final Readiness Criteria

Before recommending a student for Module 13, trainers should be satisfied that the student demonstrates reliable performance in:

Professionalism Consistent professional behaviour.
Integrity Honest acknowledgement of errors and uncertainty.
Knowledge Sufficient understanding of Academy learning.
Safety Reliable firearms, vehicle and equipment discipline.
Communication Clear, relevant information sharing.
Teamwork Effective contribution to crew and team performance.
Decision-Making Reasonable and explainable decisions.
Reassessment Recognises and adapts to meaningful changes.
Awareness Maintains awareness of the wider incident.
Accountability Can explain actions clearly afterwards.

When NOT to Recommend Final Assessment

A student should normally receive further development before Module 13 where a significant unresolved concern remains.

Examples may include:

  • Repeated safety concerns.
  • Repeated failure to communicate significant information.
  • Persistent inability to reassess changing circumstances.
  • Repeated poor team behaviour.
  • Significant professionalism concerns.
  • Persistent confusion between reports and confirmed information.
  • Failure to apply repeated trainer feedback.
  • Inability to explain important decisions.

Additional development should be specific to the identified concern.

Module 12 Outcomes

Ready for Final Assessment

Student demonstrates sufficiently consistent performance and is recommended for Module 13.

Ready with Development Points

Student may progress, but identified development points should be recorded and considered during final assessment.

Further Development Required

Student is not yet recommended for Module 13 and requires targeted training.

Incomplete

Required Module 12 development work remains outstanding.

Final Readiness Discussion

Before completing Module 12, speak to the student directly.

Suggested wording — Ready

“You have now completed the development phase of the ARU Academy.

Across Module 12 you have demonstrated that you can apply previous learning consistently, respond to changing circumstances and apply feedback.

You are being recommended to progress to Module 13 — Final Assessment.”

Suggested wording — Further Development

“You have completed the majority of the development work required, but there is one area I want to strengthen before final assessment.

This is not a failure. The purpose of Module 12 is to identify these issues before the final assessment.

We will complete targeted development in this area and review your readiness again afterwards.”

Common Student Errors

  • Trying to impress rather than performing consistently.
  • Rushing because final assessment is approaching.
  • Becoming defensive when older development points are revisited.
  • Memorising scenarios instead of applying principles.
  • Failing to communicate uncertainty.
  • Repeating a previously identified error.
  • Ignoring the wider team.
  • Continuing with an outdated plan.
  • Failing to reflect honestly during debrief.

Common Trainer Errors

  • Treating Module 12 as the final assessment.
  • Introducing lots of new theory.
  • Making scenarios difficult simply for the sake of difficulty.
  • Failing to review previous training records.
  • Giving vague development feedback.
  • Failing to provide an opportunity to apply feedback.
  • Using the same development scenario repeatedly.
  • Recommending progression despite an unresolved significant concern.
  • Holding a student back for minor imperfections that do not affect overall competence.
  • Failing to record the reasons for the readiness decision.

Closing Script

Suggested wording

“Module 12 concludes the training and development phase of the Academy.

You have now been taught the standards, given opportunities to practise them and received feedback across the course.

Module 13 is different. It is the point where you demonstrate that you can apply those standards independently and consistently.

Use the Academy pages to revise any areas you are unsure about before final assessment.”
