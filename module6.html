Module 6 — Firearms Fundamentals & Operational Readiness

Code44 Police Service

Armed Response Unit Academy

Cohort 001

Module Overview

Module 6 develops the safety principles introduced during Module 5 and begins preparing students to operate responsibly as armed officers within Code44.

The focus of this module is not simply the possession of a firearm. Students must demonstrate discipline, communication, judgement, awareness and accountability while operating as part of an ARU crew.

Students will complete trainer-led practical exercises using the Code44 FiveM firearms system and simulated training scenarios.

Training Phase Firearms Development
Delivery Trainer-led practical training
Assessment Continuous observation and knowledge check
Progression Required before Module 7

Important

Module 6 is designed around Code44 FiveM roleplay and the server's firearms mechanics.

Practical exercises must be conducted under the direction of an approved ARU trainer.

Students are not expected to arrive already knowing how every exercise works. They are expected to listen, learn, communicate and apply trainer feedback.

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of Module 6, students should be able to:

  • Explain what operational readiness means within ARU.
  • Identify the normal roles of the P226 and SIG MCX.
  • Demonstrate disciplined behaviour while armed in simulation.
  • Understand that ARU status does not mean a firearm must always be deployed.
  • Communicate clearly with their ARU partner.
  • Maintain awareness of other officers during practical exercises.
  • Recognise changing circumstances within a scenario.
  • Reassess decisions when the threat or information changes.
  • Use Code44 firearms mechanics safely and appropriately.
  • Respond correctly when equipment or server mechanics fail.
  • Maintain accountability for issued equipment.
  • Accept and apply trainer feedback.

2. Operational Readiness

Operational readiness means more than having the correct equipment.

An ARU officer must be mentally prepared, appropriately equipped, aware of their responsibilities and capable of working effectively with their crew.

Equipment Required equipment should be available, accounted for and ready for the role being performed.
Information Officers should understand the information available to them and identify important gaps.
Communication Crew members should communicate clearly and understand their responsibilities.
Professionalism Officers must remain disciplined regardless of how routine or exciting an incident appears.
Awareness Officers should remain aware of colleagues, subjects and the wider environment.
Decision-Making Decisions should reflect the circumstances that actually exist, not assumptions about what may happen.

ARV Crewing

Code44 ARVs are normally double-crewed by trained ARU officers.

Operating as a crew means communicating, sharing information and supporting each other's awareness.

Two officers sitting in the same vehicle does not automatically make them an effective team.

3. Firearms Roles

Students were introduced to Code44 firearms during previous modules. Module 6 focuses primarily on the standard operational platforms they will encounter as ARU officers.

SIG Sauer P226 The P226 is the personal sidearm available to trained and authorised ARU officers. Personal Sidearm
SIG MCX The MCX is the primary operational carbine available for authorised ARU deployments. Primary Carbine
SIG 716 The SIG 716 provides a specialist precision capability. Detailed specialist training sits outside this stage of the initial ARU Academy. Specialist Capability
Remington The Remington is a specialist breaching platform. It is not the routine primary firearm for ordinary ARU deployments. Breaching Capability

4. Deploying a Firearm

Being an ARU officer does not mean that a firearm should automatically be deployed at every incident.

Students must develop the judgement to recognise that the presence of armed officers and the deployment of firearms are separate considerations.

Decisions should reflect:

  • The information currently available.
  • The behaviour being displayed.
  • Known or suspected weapons.
  • The potential threat to officers or others.
  • The environment.
  • The role assigned to the officer.
  • Instructions or command decisions.
  • Changes in circumstances.

Do Not Operate on Autopilot

“Armed job” does not mean every decision has already been made.

Students must continue observing, communicating and reassessing throughout an incident.

What was appropriate thirty seconds ago may no longer be appropriate after the circumstances change.

5. Appropriate Firearm Selection

Students should understand the intended role of available equipment and follow trainer or command direction regarding its use.

Selection should not be based upon:

  • Which firearm looks more impressive.
  • Personal preference alone.
  • What another officer happens to be carrying.
  • A desire to escalate the roleplay.

Students should instead consider the assigned role, available information and direction provided by trainers or commanders.

6. Communication

Communication is one of the most important skills an ARU student will develop.

Officers cannot assume that their partner has seen, heard or understood the same information.

Useful communication may include:

  • Relevant observations.
  • Changes in subject behaviour.
  • Important information received over the radio.
  • Changes in the environment.
  • Equipment problems.
  • Uncertainty or missing information.
  • Changes to the team's role.
  • Information relevant to other officers.
Remember Good communication is clear, relevant and timely. Talking constantly is not the same as communicating effectively.

ARU Radio Channels

ARU officers normally operate on the main operational radio channel alongside other units.

ARU also has access to its own channel for training or operations when required.

Students must listen to trainer instructions regarding which channel should be used during an exercise.

7. Team Awareness

Firearms deployments are team activities.

Students must remain aware of where other officers are positioned and how their own actions may affect them.

During practical training, consider:

  • Where is my ARU partner?
  • Where are other officers?
  • Has somebody moved?
  • Has another unit entered the scene?
  • Has my assigned responsibility changed?
  • Am I still receiving radio information?
  • Do I need to communicate something?

Tunnel vision is something trainers will actively look for during practical exercises.

8. Threat Identification

Students should separate what they know from what they suspect.

Known Information that has been established within the scenario.
Reported Information supplied by another person or source.
Observed Information personally seen or heard by the officer.
Suspected Something that may be true but has not yet been confirmed.

Students should be able to explain which information influenced their decisions rather than simply saying that they “thought” something was happening.

9. Reassessment

An incident is not frozen at the moment ARU arrives.

Information and behaviour can change rapidly.

Students should continuously consider:

  • Has the subject's behaviour changed?
  • Has new information been received?
  • Has a suspected weapon been confirmed or disproved?
  • Has the threat increased?
  • Has the threat reduced?
  • Have members of the public entered or left the area?
  • Have additional officers arrived?
  • Has command direction changed?
  • Does my current action remain appropriate?

De-escalation Is Still Success

A firearms deployment does not need to end with force for ARU to have performed effectively.

If circumstances change and the threat reduces, officers should be capable of adapting accordingly.

Professional ARU officers are judged by their decision-making and control — not by how much force they use.

10. Code44 Firearms Mechanics

Trainers will provide supervised familiarisation with the firearms mechanics used on the Code44 server.

The purpose of this stage is to ensure that students understand how the server behaves before those mechanics are introduced into more demanding operational scenarios.

Students may be assessed on their ability to:

  • Follow trainer instructions.
  • Use the appropriate server controls during simulation.
  • Maintain awareness while interacting with equipment.
  • Recognise when something has not worked correctly.
  • Communicate technical problems immediately.
  • Return to a safe training state when instructed.

11. Equipment and Script Problems

FiveM mechanics do not always behave as expected.

If equipment, animations, inventory systems or another server mechanic behaves unexpectedly during training, students should not attempt to hide the problem or continue regardless.

The correct approach is:

  1. Stop where appropriate.
  2. Communicate the issue.
  3. Follow trainer direction.
  4. Allow the exercise to be reset if required.
Training Principle A technical issue is not a student failure. Failing to communicate a known problem may become a training issue.

12. Post-Exercise Accountability

The end of a scenario does not mean the officer's responsibilities immediately disappear.

Students should ensure that:

  • Issued equipment remains accounted for.
  • Long firearms are returned to the ARV when no longer required.
  • Firearms are secured appropriately.
  • Equipment problems are reported.
  • Relevant information is passed to the trainer.
  • Students participate properly in the debrief.

ARV Firearms Storage

All long firearms carried within Code44 ARVs must remain secured in the rear firearms-storage area when they are not authorised for deployment.

The normal exception is the trained officer's P226 personal sidearm.

Practical Training

Module 6 contains a significant practical element.

Trainers will use controlled exercises to assess how students combine the knowledge learned throughout Modules 1–6.

Practical training may assess:

  • Professional preparation.
  • Communication.
  • Team awareness.
  • Firearms discipline.
  • Response to trainer commands.
  • Threat identification.
  • Reassessment.
  • Equipment accountability.
  • Ability to accept feedback.

Scenario details and assessment criteria are intentionally not provided within the student material.

What Trainers Are Looking For

Module 6 is not a competition to see who can complete an exercise fastest.

Students should demonstrate:

  • Control.
  • Awareness.
  • Communication.
  • Judgement.
  • Professionalism.
  • Accountability.
  • Willingness to learn.

A student who slows down and asks for clarification may demonstrate better judgement than a student who rushes forward without understanding the situation.

13. Module 6 Revision

Before completing the Module 6 knowledge check, students should be comfortable answering the following:

  1. What does operational readiness mean for an ARU officer?
  2. How are Code44 ARVs normally crewed?
  3. What is the normal role of the P226?
  4. What is the normal role of the SIG MCX?
  5. Why are the SIG 716 and Remington treated differently?
  6. Does attending an armed incident automatically mean a firearm must remain deployed throughout?
  7. Why is communication between ARU crew members important?
  8. What is the difference between known, reported, observed and suspected information?
  9. Why must officers continually reassess an incident?
  10. What should you do when a Code44 server mechanic does not behave as expected?
  11. Where should long firearms be stored when they are no longer required?
  12. What should happen after a practical exercise finishes?

Progression Standard

Successful completion of Module 6 requires more than passing the knowledge check.

Students must demonstrate satisfactory performance in:

  • Module 6 student learning completed
  • Trainer-led practical training completed
  • Firearms safety standards maintained
  • Appropriate communication demonstrated
  • Team awareness demonstrated
  • Threat information assessed appropriately
  • Changing circumstances recognised
  • Trainer commands followed
  • Server mechanics understood sufficiently
  • Equipment accountability maintained
  • Knowledge check completed
  • Trainer sign-off received

Looking Ahead — Module 7

Module 7 moves from individual operational readiness into ARV Deployment & Armed Incident Response.

Students will begin applying their existing knowledge as part of an ARV crew responding to developing armed incidents.

The standards introduced throughout Modules 1–6 remain in force throughout every later Academy module.

Module 6 Knowledge Check

Complete the knowledge check after finishing the student material and trainer-led training.

The knowledge check confirms understanding of the principles covered during Module 6. Practical performance is assessed separately by the trainer.
