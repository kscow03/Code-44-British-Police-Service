Trainer Module 13

Final Assessment & Qualification

Code44 Armed Response Unit Academy

RESTRICTED FINAL ASSESSMENT MATERIAL

Final Assessment Overview

Module 13 is the final assessment stage of the Code44 ARU Academy. Unlike Modules 1–12, this module is primarily an assessment rather than a teaching session.

Students should be required to demonstrate that they can independently apply the standards developed throughout the Academy without excessive trainer intervention.

The assessment should not use one permanent scenario. Trainers should select different exercises from the assessment bank so that students are assessed against the same standards while encountering different circumstances.

Assessment Type Final ARU Qualification Assessment
Assessment Style Knowledge + practical + scenario + debrief
Scenario System Trainer-selected assessment bank
Coaching Minimal during assessed elements
Standard Consistent independent performance
Final Decision Qualification recommendation

Required Final Assessment Structure

Each student should complete a balanced assessment containing several different elements.

Element Requirement Purpose
Element A Knowledge Assessment Confirm understanding of Academy learning.
Element B Equipment / Readiness Check Confirm understanding of ARU resources and accountability.
Element C Decision-Making Exercise Test information handling, proportionality and reassessment.
Element D Operational Scenario 1 General ARV / armed incident assessment.
Element E Operational Scenario 2 Different operational environment.
Element F Complex / Integrated Scenario Combine multiple Academy learning areas.
Element G Post-Incident Handover Assess information retention and communication.
Element H Final Debrief Assess accountability and reasoning.

Academy Coverage Requirement

The final assessment should collectively test learning from across the entire Academy.

Professional Standards Integrity, attitude, accountability and conduct.
Decision-Making Information, threat, risk, proportionality and reassessment.
Equipment PPE, vehicles, firearms availability and accountability.
Firearms Safety Safe simulated handling and trainer-command compliance.
Operational Readiness Preparation before and during ARV deployment.
ARV Response Crew responsibilities and armed incident response.
Vehicle Operations High-risk vehicle incidents and coordinated vehicle operations.
Containment & Search Role awareness and coordinated search activity.
Building Operations Communication, team discipline and scenario awareness.
Integrated Operations NPAS, K9 and wider police-resource awareness.
Complex Incidents Multiple information streams and changing priorities.
Accountability Debrief, explanation and honest reflection.

Scenario Selection System

Do not give every student the same final assessment.

For each candidate, trainers should normally select:

  • 1 knowledge assessment.
  • 1 readiness/equipment test.
  • 1 short decision-making test.
  • 2 operational scenarios from different categories.
  • 1 complex integrated scenario.
  • 1 post-incident handover.
  • 1 final accountability debrief.

Scenarios can also be modified using the inject bank later on this page.

Important: The objective is not to make one student's assessment harder than another. Different scenarios should still test comparable Academy standards.

Element A — Final Knowledge Assessment

Trainers may select questions from the following bank rather than asking every student the exact same questions.

Knowledge Question Bank
  1. What does integrity mean within ARU?
  2. Why is accountability particularly important for an armed officer?
  3. Explain the difference between reported and confirmed information.
  4. Why must an operational assessment remain continuous?
  5. What should happen if circumstances materially change?
  6. What factors should influence whether an action remains proportionate?
  7. What should an officer do after making a mistake?
  8. Why is communication important within an ARV crew?
  9. What responsibility does an officer have for issued equipment?
  10. Where should firearms normally be secured within an ARV when not carried?
  11. What firearm is normally carried as the ARU sidearm?
  12. What is the primary ARU rifle within Code44?
  13. What is the Remington shotgun used for within Code44 ARU?
  14. What PPE may be available to an ARU officer?
  15. Why should equipment selection be based on operational need?
  16. Why should information uncertainty be communicated?
  17. What should happen when an existing plan no longer fits the circumstances?
  18. Why should an ARU officer consider the wider incident rather than only the immediate subject?
  19. What is expected during an operational handover?
  20. What should an officer be capable of explaining following an incident?
  21. Why might NPAS be requested?
  22. Why might K9 support be requested?
  23. Why must specialist resources have a clear operational purpose?
  24. What should an officer do if a server mechanic does not work as expected during an exercise?
  25. Why does qualification not mean development has finished?

Element B — Equipment & Operational Readiness

The trainer should give the student a hypothetical deployment and ask them to identify appropriate Code44 equipment and considerations.

Readiness Test A — Standard ARV Deployment

Student is told they are deploying as part of a double-crewed ARV.

Ask them to explain:

  • What equipment they would expect to have available.
  • What PPE may be relevant.
  • Where firearms not being carried should remain.
  • What responsibility they have for equipment.
  • How they would communicate with their crew.
  • Which radio channel may be appropriate depending on the operation.
Readiness Test B — Reduced Information Deployment

Provide only limited information about a reported armed subject.

Assess whether the student distinguishes between what they know, what has merely been reported and what remains unknown.

Readiness Test C — Specialist Equipment Questioning

Ask the student when additional equipment such as the ballistic shield, gas mask, NVG or specialist resources might be considered.

The important assessment point is whether the student links equipment selection to the circumstances rather than automatically selecting everything.

Element C — Decision-Making Tests

Decision Test 1 — Conflicting Information

Control reports that a male may have a firearm.

Another officer states over radio that they believe the object may instead be a mobile phone.

Ask:

  • What information is confirmed?
  • What remains reported?
  • What information would you seek?
  • How does the uncertainty affect your thinking?
Decision Test 2 — Changing Threat

The initial information suggests an armed and threatening subject.

Inject: The subject subsequently becomes compliant and follows police instructions.

Assess whether the student recognises that the circumstances have changed and reassesses their response.

Decision Test 3 — Escalating Information

Initial deployment concerns a suspicious person.

Inject: Control provides new information that a weapon has now been reported.

Assess whether the student recognises the significance of the new information, communicates it and updates their assessment.

Decision Test 4 — Incorrect Initial Information

The student receives information that a particular vehicle is believed to contain the subject.

Inject: Control later confirms the registration was incorrectly reported.

Assess whether the student stops relying on outdated information.

Operational Scenario Bank

The following scenarios can be selected and modified for individual final assessments.

Trainers should not reveal which scenarios will be used beforehand.

Scenario 1 — Reported Armed Person

Response officers request ARU following reports of a person seen with what may be a firearm.

Starting Information

  • One reporting person.
  • Weapon has been reported but not independently confirmed.
  • Subject description available.
  • Last known location available.
  • Members of the public remain nearby.

Assessment Focus

Information Handling Communication Risk Awareness Reassessment Teamwork

Possible Inject A: A second caller reports the object may have been a phone.
Possible Inject B: Another officer reports seeing an object but cannot identify it.
Possible Inject C: The subject voluntarily approaches police.
Expected Standard: Student distinguishes reports from confirmed facts, communicates uncertainty, works with their crew and adjusts their assessment as information changes.

Scenario 2 — Armed Vehicle Report

A vehicle is reported to contain an individual believed to possess a weapon.

Starting Information

  • Vehicle description available.
  • Registration partially confirmed.
  • Number of occupants unknown.
  • Weapon information remains reported.

Assessment Focus

Vehicle Awareness Information Communication Team Coordination

Possible Inject A: A second similar vehicle appears.
Possible Inject B: Control corrects part of the registration.
Possible Inject C: The vehicle stops and an occupant becomes compliant.
Expected Standard: Student avoids treating unconfirmed information as fact and maintains effective communication with the ARV crew and wider operation.

Scenario 3 — High-Risk Stop Assessment

ARU is supporting an operation involving a vehicle believed to contain a potentially dangerous subject.

This scenario assesses the Academy's simulated vehicle-operation principles and should be delivered using Code44 server mechanics and established training rules.

Assessment Focus

Role Discipline Communication Teamwork Reassessment

Possible Inject: The original subject is discovered not to be inside the vehicle.
Expected Standard: Student recognises that the operational picture has changed rather than continuing simply because an original plan existed.

Scenario 4 — Failed Vehicle Containment

A vehicle operation is underway when the target vehicle unexpectedly moves away from the planned location.

Assessment Focus

Adaptability Radio Communication Awareness Decision-Making

Possible Inject: A civilian vehicle becomes involved in the area.
Expected Standard: Student communicates the material change and does not remain mentally fixed on the original plan.

Scenario 5 — Armed Containment

A subject believed to be armed enters a location and ARU establishes a simulated containment.

Assessment Focus

Role Awareness Containment Communication Patience

Possible Inject A: A civilian leaves the location.
Possible Inject B: Information suggests there may be another exit.
Possible Inject C: NPAS becomes available.
Expected Standard: Student maintains their assigned role, communicates relevant changes and considers available supporting resources.

Scenario 6 — Search Operation

ARU is tasked with supporting a simulated search for a potentially armed subject.

Assessment Focus

Briefing Teamwork Information Resource Awareness

Possible Inject: K9 becomes available during the operation.
Expected Standard: Student considers whether the resource contributes to the operational objective rather than requesting it automatically.

Scenario 7 — High-Risk Warrant

ARU is supporting a simulated high-risk warrant involving a subject reported to have access to firearms.

Assessment Focus

Briefing Information Role Discipline Team Communication

Possible Inject A: The target is not present.
Possible Inject B: An unexpected person is present.
Possible Inject C: New intelligence changes the original understanding of the incident.
Expected Standard: Student responds to the scenario actually presented rather than attempting to force the original expectation.

Scenario 8 — Building Incident

Officers respond to a simulated armed incident within a Code44 building environment.

Assessment Focus

Communication Team Discipline Awareness Decision-Making

Possible Inject A: Information suggests the subject may have moved.
Possible Inject B: A civilian is located.
Possible Inject C: The reported subject description changes.
Expected Standard: Student maintains communication and team awareness and responds appropriately to changing scenario information.

Scenario 9 — Multiple Subjects

Initial information concerns one subject. During the exercise, information indicates a possible second person of interest.

Assessment Focus

Information Management Tunnel Vision Communication Reassessment

Expected Standard: Student incorporates the new information into the wider operational picture rather than remaining focused solely on the original subject.

Scenario 10 — Civilian Complication

A simulated armed operation is underway when a member of the public unexpectedly enters the scenario.

Assessment Focus

Public Safety Awareness Communication Adaptability

Expected Standard: Student recognises the new risk and incorporates it into their decision-making.

Scenario 11 — Casualty During Operation

During a wider simulated incident, a casualty is introduced.

Assessment Focus

Prioritisation Communication TacMed Awareness Wider Incident Awareness

Possible Inject: The original operational threat has not yet been fully resolved.
Expected Standard: Student recognises both the casualty requirement and the continuing wider incident rather than developing tunnel vision on only one problem.

Scenario 12 — NPAS Integration

NPAS becomes available during a simulated search or containment operation.

Assessment Focus

Resource Integration Communication Operational Purpose

Possible Inject: NPAS reports information that conflicts with an earlier witness report.
Expected Standard: Student considers the reliability and significance of new information and updates the operational picture appropriately.

Scenario 13 — K9 Integration

A K9 resource becomes available during a simulated search operation.

Assessment Focus

Resource Awareness Communication Team Integration

Expected Standard: Student understands that specialist resources should support a defined operational objective rather than simply being used because available.

Scenario 14 — Conflicting Witness Accounts

Two witnesses provide different descriptions of the same incident.

Assessment Focus

Information Handling Uncertainty Decision-Making

Expected Standard: Student does not arbitrarily select one account as fact and clearly communicates what remains uncertain.

Scenario 15 — Wrong Subject

A person initially believed to match the suspect description is encountered.

Inject: New information demonstrates that the individual is not the subject being sought.

Assessment Focus

Reassessment Information Professionalism

Expected Standard: Student responds to the corrected information and does not attempt to justify continuing an outdated assessment.

Scenario 16 — Communication Failure

During an incident, another simulated unit misses an important radio update.

Assessment Focus

Communication Team Awareness Initiative

Expected Standard: Student recognises that another team may be operating with outdated information and takes reasonable steps to ensure relevant information is shared.

Scenario 17 — Server Mechanic Failure

During an assessment scenario, a FiveM mechanic, animation, vehicle feature or scripted item does not function correctly.

Assessment Focus

Adaptability Communication Professionalism

Expected Standard: Student communicates the issue and continues through reasonable roleplay rather than allowing a technical problem to destroy the scenario.

Scenario 18 — Command Update

The student has begun operating under an established plan.

Inject: Command changes the operational objective following new information.

Assessment Focus

Adaptability Communication Role Discipline

Expected Standard: Student acknowledges and works toward the updated objective rather than continuing independently with the previous plan.

Scenario 19 — Specialist Capability Decision

The trainer presents a complex operation and tells the student that several specialist Code44 capabilities are available.

These may include:

  • NPAS.
  • K9.
  • Ballistic shield.
  • Specialist breaching capability.
  • Maritime capability.
  • Rope access.
  • Fast-rope capability.

Assessment Focus

Resource Selection Justification Operational Purpose

Expected Standard: Student selects resources because they contribute to the scenario objective, not because they are available or appear impressive.

Scenario 20 — Multi-Location Incident

Information begins arriving about potentially related activity at two locations.

Assessment Focus

Information Management Prioritisation Communication Wider Awareness

Possible Inject: Information later suggests the incidents are unrelated.
Expected Standard: Student manages uncertainty and does not create an unsupported connection between incidents.

Scenario 21 — VIP Protection Complication

During a simulated protection task, an unrelated disturbance develops nearby.

Assessment Focus

Role Discipline Prioritisation Communication

Expected Standard: Student remembers the assigned operational responsibility and communicates appropriately rather than abandoning their role simply to become involved in another incident.

Scenario 22 — Maritime Support Decision

A simulated incident develops near or around a maritime environment and specialist capability may become relevant.

Assessment Focus

Specialist Awareness Resource Request Decision-Making

Expected Standard: Student identifies when the incident is moving beyond ordinary ARV capability and appropriately considers specialist support.

Scenario 23 — Counter-Terrorism Style Multi-Agency Incident

A simulated major incident produces multiple reports, several police resources and rapidly changing information.

This scenario is intended to assess coordination and information management, not specialist CT tactics.

Assessment Focus

Major Incident Awareness Information Role Discipline Communication

Possible Inject A: A second report is received from another location.
Possible Inject B: One early report is later shown to be incorrect.
Possible Inject C: Additional specialist resources become available.
Expected Standard: Student remains within their assigned role, communicates clearly and does not attempt to independently control an incident beyond their role.

Scenario 24 — Full Academy Integration

This is the highest-complexity scenario within the assessment bank.

The trainer creates a FiveM incident combining several previous modules.

Possible Components

  • Initial incomplete deployment information.
  • ARV response.
  • Vehicle involvement.
  • Reported weapon.
  • Multiple persons.
  • Containment.
  • Building element.
  • Civilian complication.
  • Casualty.
  • NPAS or K9 availability.
  • Conflicting information.
  • Command update.
  • Final handover.
Trainer Rule: Do not use every possible inject. Select enough changes to assess the student while keeping the scenario understandable and fair.

Weapon Disproved The reported weapon is shown not to be a weapon.
Subject Complies Subject behaviour changes significantly.
Subject Moves The last known location changes.
Second Subject Another person of interest is reported.
Civilian Appears Public-safety considerations change.
Casualty A simulated casualty is introduced.
NPAS Available Air support becomes available.
K9 Available Dog support becomes available.
Comms Problem Another unit misses an important update.
Vehicle Change Vehicle information changes.

New Priority Another operational issue requires consideration.

Element G — Post-Incident Handover

Following at least one practical scenario, require the student to provide a clear handover without being given the answers by the trainer.

The handover should demonstrate awareness of:

  • What the original incident was.
  • What information was received.
  • What was confirmed.
  • What remained uncertain.
  • Significant changes during the incident.
  • Actions taken.
  • Current subject status.
  • Casualties where relevant.
  • Outstanding risks.
  • Outstanding actions.
Expected Standard: The handover does not need to be word-perfect. It should allow another officer or commander to understand the current operational picture.

Element H — Final Accountability Debrief

After the practical assessment, ask the student to explain their own decisions.

  1. What did you understand the incident to be when you were deployed?
  2. What information did you consider confirmed?
  3. What remained uncertain?
  4. What was your main concern?
  5. What options did you consider?
  6. Why did you choose the actions you took?
  7. What changed during the scenario?
  8. How did those changes affect your decisions?
  9. What do you think you did well?
  10. What would you change if completing the scenario again?
Trainer Guidance: Do not expect students to invent perfect retrospective answers. The purpose is to determine whether their reasoning is honest, coherent and consistent with the information available during the scenario.

Final Assessment Marking Framework

Each category should be considered across the whole assessment rather than based on one isolated moment.

Category Required Standard
Professionalism Consistent professional behaviour throughout.
Integrity Honest about actions, errors and uncertainty.
Knowledge Demonstrates sufficient understanding of Academy learning.
Information Handling Separates reported, confirmed and unknown information.
Communication Passes important information clearly and appropriately.
Teamwork Works effectively with crew members and other resources.
Decision-Making Makes reasonable and explainable decisions.
Reassessment Responds appropriately when circumstances change.
Operational Awareness Maintains awareness of the wider incident.
Safety Maintains expected Code44 simulated firearms/equipment standards.
Resource Awareness Uses or requests resources for an operational purpose.
Handover Provides a useful and accurate operational summary.
Accountability Can explain decisions and reflect on performance.

Performance Ratings

EXCEEDS STANDARD Performance is consistently above the level required of a newly qualified Code44 ARU officer.
MEETS STANDARD Performance demonstrates the required level for qualification.
DEVELOPMENT REQUIRED A weakness exists but may be suitable for targeted development or reassessment.
BELOW STANDARD Performance does not currently demonstrate the required level for qualification.

Serious / Critical Assessment Concerns

Not every mistake should result in automatic failure.

However, trainers should immediately record and review significant concerns such as:

  • Repeated unsafe simulated firearms behaviour.
  • Ignoring clear trainer safety commands.
  • Dishonesty during assessment or debrief.
  • Attempting to conceal a significant mistake.
  • Repeated inability to distinguish reported information from confirmed fact.
  • Repeated failure to communicate significant changes.
  • Continuing with an obviously outdated plan despite clear new information.
  • Serious professionalism or conduct concerns.
  • Persistent inability to work within the ARV team.
  • Performance showing that previous Academy learning has not been retained.

The trainer should consider the whole assessment and the seriousness of the issue before determining the final outcome.

Assessment Fairness

Trainers must not:

  • Deliberately create impossible scenarios.
  • Change rules midway through an assessment.
  • Expect students to know information they were never given.
  • Penalise a student for FiveM/server problems outside their control.
  • Require one exact answer where several reasonable responses exist.
  • Give one student substantially easier standards than another.
  • Provide excessive coaching during assessed elements.
  • Fail a student merely because they did something differently from the trainer.

Final Qualification Outcomes

PASS — QUALIFIED

Student demonstrates the required standard across the final assessment and is recommended for qualification as a Code44 ARU officer.

PASS — DEVELOPMENT POINTS

Student meets the qualification standard but has limited development points that should continue to be monitored.

REASSESSMENT REQUIRED

A specific assessment area requires further testing before a qualification decision can be confirmed.

FURTHER TRAINING REQUIRED

Student requires targeted development before another final assessment attempt.

Qualification Recommendation

Where the student successfully completes the assessment, the trainer should complete the official ARU Academy training record.

The record should include:

  • Officer name.
  • Call-sign / collar number.
  • Date of final assessment.
  • Assessing trainer.
  • Assessment elements completed.
  • Overall performance summary.
  • Any remaining development points.
  • Final assessment outcome.
  • Qualification recommendation.
Record Final Assessment Return to Trainers Area

Academy Completion

Successful completion of Module 13 marks completion of the Code44 Armed Response Unit Academy.

The officer may then be recorded as having completed the core ARU qualification process, subject to the relevant Code44 authorisation.

Qualification should represent the beginning of continued operational development rather than the end of training.
