Trainer Module 10 — Building Operations & CQB

Instructor Lesson Plan

ARU Academy • Cohort 001

Trainer-Only Resource

Lesson Overview

Module 10 develops the containment and search principles introduced in Module 9 and applies them to armed team operations within buildings and confined Code44 environments.

Students should demonstrate controlled team behaviour, clear communication, role discipline, situational awareness, information handling, threat reassessment and appropriate use of specialist resources.

Practical delivery should use controlled FiveM scenarios and the current Code44 server mechanics.

Lesson Module 10 — Building Operations & CQB
Recommended Duration 120–180 minutes
Delivery Style Briefing, demonstrations, controlled building scenarios and debrief
Assessment Continuous practical observation and knowledge check
Primary Focus Team coordination, communication, awareness and reassessment
Progression Required before Module 11

Mandatory Training Boundary

Module 10 is designed for Code44 FiveM roleplay and server-based training.

Trainers should focus on communication, team roles, awareness, information handling, specialist-resource integration, reassessment and controlled scenario performance.

This Academy material does not provide real-world room-clearing methods, exact entry geometry, weapon manipulation instruction or physical CQB procedures.

Practical demonstrations should use the current Code44 environment and server mechanics only.

Trainer Preparation

  • Confirm students completed Module 9
  • Confirm students have read Module 10
  • Prepare a suitable Code44 building or training location
  • Prepare at least one simple building scenario
  • Prepare a more complex multi-room scenario
  • Prepare roleplayers where available
  • Prepare civilian or unknown-person injects
  • Prepare changing weapon information
  • Prepare ballistic shield familiarisation where appropriate
  • Prepare breaching-capability discussion or demonstration
  • Prepare K9 or NPAS integration where available
  • Prepare a casualty scenario
  • Prepare a change-of-objective scenario
  • Prepare a communication-failure scenario
  • Prepare the Module 10 knowledge check
  • Prepare observation and assessment notes

Learning Outcomes

By the end of Module 10, students should be able to:

  • Explain the purpose of controlled building operations.
  • Understand the operational objective before beginning a scenario.
  • Identify relevant intelligence and important unknowns.
  • Understand their assigned team role.
  • Communicate relevant changes clearly.
  • Maintain awareness of other team members.
  • Recognise civilians and persons of unknown involvement.
  • Understand the broad purpose of the ballistic shield.
  • Understand the Remington as a specialist breaching capability.
  • Recognise when specialist resources may assist.
  • Recognise when the original plan is no longer appropriate.
  • Respond appropriately to changing threat information.
  • Complete a clear post-operation handover.
  • Explain decisions during debrief.

Opening Script

Suggested wording

“Module 10 moves us into building operations and CQB-based scenarios.

The focus is not who can move through the building fastest. The focus is whether the team understands the objective, communicates, remains aware of each other and adapts when new information appears.

You will be assessed on control, teamwork, awareness, decision-making and how well you understand your role within the wider operation.”

Stage 1 — Building Operation Principles

Explain that confined environments add complexity because students may have incomplete information regarding subjects, civilians and location.

Before beginning, students should identify:

  • The operational objective.
  • Who is believed to be involved.
  • Weapon information.
  • Known civilians or occupants.
  • Important unknowns.
  • Available supporting resources.
  • The immediate reason for entering or searching.
  • What circumstances may cause the plan to change.

Exercise One — Pre-Operation Brief

Scenario ARU is tasked with searching a commercial building following reports that an armed suspect entered the location. Several employees may still be inside. The firearm has been reported but not personally confirmed by attending officers.

Ask students:

  • What is the operational objective?
  • What information is confirmed?
  • What remains reported?
  • What remains unknown?
  • Who may be at risk?
  • What resources might assist?

Expected learning points

  • The firearm report remains relevant but may not be confirmed.
  • Employees or civilians may still be inside.
  • The subject's exact location may be unknown.
  • Students should not invent missing information.
  • The operation should remain flexible.

Stage 2 — Team Roles

Explain that the exact structure depends on the Code44 scenario, available officers and trainer direction.

Team Lead Supports coordination and ensures the immediate objective is understood.
Team Members Carry out assigned tasks while maintaining awareness and communication.
Shield Capability May be incorporated where appropriate to the scenario.
Breaching Capability May support authorised access where the scenario requires it.
Containment Maintains wider scene control and supports the building operation.
Command Provides wider tactical oversight where required.

Exercise Two — Role Allocation

Give students a simple operation and assign different responsibilities.

Ask each student to explain:

  • Their assigned responsibility.
  • What information they need.
  • Who they need to communicate with.
  • What may cause their role to change.

Correct students who attempt to perform every responsibility themselves.

Stage 3 — Communication

Useful communication within a building scenario may include:

  • Subject sightings.
  • Civilians located.
  • Weapon information.
  • Changes in subject behaviour.
  • Significant movement.
  • Loss of communication.
  • Medical emergencies.
  • Changes in the operational objective.
  • Technical or server problems.
Trainer Point Communication should support the team. Constant unnecessary radio or voice traffic may reduce awareness rather than improve it.

Exercise Three — Information Relay

Give each student different pieces of information during a controlled building scenario.

Assess whether:

  • Important information is passed.
  • Students listen to each other.
  • Reports are separated from direct observations.
  • Conflicting information is recognised.
  • The team updates its understanding.

Stage 4 — Controlled Team Movement

Reinforce that students should remain coordinated rather than treating the building as an individual race.

Assess:

  • Shared understanding.
  • Team awareness.
  • Communication.
  • Compliance with trainer direction.
  • Recognition of confusion.
  • Controlled progress through the scenario.
Key Teaching Point “Controlled” does not simply mean moving slowly. It means the team's actions remain coordinated and understood.

Exercise Four — Basic Building Scenario

Use a relatively simple Code44 building.

Include:

  • One known subject.
  • A simple operational objective.
  • Limited changing information.
  • At least one non-threat roleplayer.

Assess:

  • Team communication.
  • Role awareness.
  • Recognition of non-threat persons.
  • Reassessment.
  • Professional control.

Stage 5 — Civilians & Unknown Persons

Explain that not every person encountered inside the location is automatically the armed subject.

Possible persons include:

  • Employees.
  • Residents.
  • Victims.
  • Witnesses.
  • Hostages.
  • Additional suspects.
  • Persons whose involvement is unclear.

Scenario One — Unknown Person Located

Students encounter a person who was not included within the original briefing.

Ask:

  • What do you actually know about this person?
  • Are they automatically the suspect?
  • What information may help establish their role?
  • What should be communicated to the team?

Expected learning points

  • The person's involvement is initially unknown.
  • Students should not create assumptions.
  • Behaviour and available information should be assessed.
  • The wider team should receive relevant information.

Stage 6 — Ballistic Shield Capability

Explain that the shield is a specialist resource that may support selected Code44 operations.

Students should understand:

  • The shield should support the operational objective.
  • The team should understand how it is being incorporated.
  • Communication remains essential.
  • The shield does not automatically solve the wider tactical problem.
  • Current Code44 mechanics should be demonstrated by the trainer.

Exercise Five — Shield Familiarisation

Use the current Code44 shield mechanic in a controlled environment.

Assess:

  • Understanding of the purpose.
  • Communication with the wider team.
  • Awareness of other officers.
  • Ability to follow trainer direction.
  • Recognition of limitations within the server mechanic.

Stage 7 — Breaching Capability

Remind students that the Remington is used within Code44 as a specialist breaching platform.

It is not the routine operational shotgun for general firearms deployments.

Students should understand:

  • Why access is required.
  • That breaching should form part of the agreed operation.
  • That the team needs to understand when access will occur.
  • That server-specific breaching mechanics are trainer-led.
  • That Module 10 does not independently qualify a breaching specialist.

Exercise Six — Breaching Discussion

Give students a scenario where the primary access point cannot be used within the game environment.

Ask:

  • What is the operational problem?
  • What capability may solve it?
  • Who needs to know that breaching is being considered?
  • What should happen before the operation continues?

Expected learning points

  • The access problem should be understood.
  • Breaching may provide an authorised solution.
  • The wider team should understand the change.
  • The operational plan may require reassessment.

Stage 8 — Specialist Resource Integration

K9 May support searching or tracking within appropriate Code44 scenarios.
NPAS May provide external observation and wider situational awareness.
Ballistic Shield Provides an additional specialist capability.
Breaching Supports authorised access requirements.
Medical Supports casualty management.
Response May support containment, evacuations and wider scene management.

Resource Selection

Students should not request specialist resources merely because they are available.

Ask them:

“What operational problem does this resource solve?”

Stage 9 — Immediate Threat vs Planned Search

Explain that not every building incident has the same urgency.

Students should distinguish between:

  • Immediate ongoing threats.
  • Controlled armed searches.
  • Planned warrant operations.
  • Post-incident searches.

The response should reflect the scenario rather than applying one automatic CQB approach.

Scenario Two — Threat Reduces

The operation begins following reports of an armed subject. During the scenario, reliable information indicates the suspected weapon is not present and the subject begins complying.

Ask:

  • What has changed?
  • Does the current threat assessment change?
  • Does the plan remain appropriate?
  • What should be communicated?

Expected learning points

  • The threat information has changed materially.
  • The response should be reassessed.
  • The team should not simply continue the original plan automatically.
  • Significant changes should be communicated.

Scenario Three — Threat Increases

During the building operation, a previously unknown subject appears and credible new information confirms a firearm.

Ask:

  • What has changed?
  • What information is now important?
  • Who needs to know?
  • Does the current plan remain suitable?
  • What additional support may now be required?

Scenario Four — Communication Failure

A student loses contact with part of the team or a radio/server issue affects communication.

Ask:

  • Should the student simply carry on?
  • What has changed?
  • Who needs to be informed?
  • Does the operation need to pause or reset?

Expected learning points

  • The communication problem should be recognised.
  • The wider team may no longer share the same understanding.
  • The issue should be communicated where possible.
  • The trainer or coordinating officer may need to pause or reset the exercise.

Scenario Five — Casualty

A roleplayer becomes a casualty during the scenario.

Ask students:

  • What has changed?
  • Does an immediate threat remain?
  • What medical support is available?
  • Who needs updating?
  • What happens to the current operational objective?

Assess whether students recognise that casualty care and threat management may need to occur alongside wider incident control.

Stage 10 — Complex Building Scenario

Once students demonstrate satisfactory performance in the earlier exercises, run a larger scenario combining several elements.

Possible elements include:

  • Several rooms or building areas.
  • Multiple roleplayers.
  • At least one civilian.
  • Conflicting intelligence.
  • A changing subject location.
  • Specialist-resource availability.
  • A change in weapon information.
  • A casualty.
  • A change in the operational objective.

Avoid introducing every possible complication at once. The scenario should assess students rather than overwhelm them.

Stage 11 — Resolution & Handover

Once the immediate scenario is resolved, students should consider:

  • Subject status.
  • Outstanding subjects.
  • Civilians or vulnerable persons.
  • Weapons or relevant items.
  • Medical requirements.
  • Force Control or command updates.
  • Scene management.
  • Witnesses.
  • ARU equipment accountability.

Exercise Seven — Operational Handover

Require each student to provide a concise handover.

The handover should include:

  • The original task.
  • What the student personally observed.
  • Subject status.
  • Weapon information.
  • Civilians or other occupants.
  • Any force used within the scenario.
  • Medical issues.
  • Outstanding risks.
  • Further action required.

Stage 12 — Debrief

Ask each student:

  • What information did you have?
  • What remained unknown?
  • What was your role?
  • What did you communicate?
  • What changed during the operation?
  • How did your decisions change?
  • What went well?
  • What would you do differently?
  • What feedback will you apply next time?

Practical Assessment Standard

A satisfactory performance should demonstrate:

  • Understanding of the operational objective.
  • Accurate information handling.
  • Recognition of important unknowns.
  • Clear role understanding.
  • Effective team communication.
  • Awareness of other officers.
  • Recognition of civilians and unknown persons.
  • Controlled team behaviour.
  • Appropriate specialist-resource awareness.
  • Recognition of changing circumstances.
  • Ability to reassess and adapt.
  • Appropriate response to communication or server problems.
  • Clear post-operation handover.
  • Honest debrief and accountability.

Review Questions

  1. What is the main purpose of a building operation?
  2. What information should be understood before the operation begins?
  3. Why is role allocation important?
  4. What information should be communicated during the operation?
  5. Why is team awareness important?
  6. How should officers treat persons whose involvement is unknown?
  7. What is the purpose of the ballistic shield capability?
  8. What role does the Remington have within Code44?
  9. When should specialist resources be used?
  10. What is the difference between an immediate threat and a planned search?
  11. What should happen when the situation materially changes?
  12. What should happen if communications fail?
  13. What should an officer be able to explain afterwards?

Expected Answers

  1. To manage the identified threat while maintaining team control, communication and awareness.
  2. Objective, subjects, weapon information, occupants, location, relevant intelligence, resources and important unknowns.
  3. So each officer understands their responsibility and contributes to one coordinated operation.
  4. Subject sightings, civilians, weapon information, significant movement, medical emergencies, communication failures and changes to the plan.
  5. Because officers must understand where colleagues and other persons are and avoid becoming focused solely on the suspected threat.
  6. As unknown until further information or behaviour allows a better assessment.
  7. To provide an additional specialist protective capability where it supports the operational objective.
  8. It is the specialist breaching platform within Code44 ARU.
  9. When they address a specific operational need or support the agreed objective.
  10. An immediate threat may require urgent action, while planned searches may allow more time, information and preparation.
  11. Reassess, communicate and modify, pause or abandon the existing plan where necessary.
  12. Recognise and communicate the issue and follow trainer or coordinating officer direction regarding whether the exercise continues.
  13. Available information, perceived threat, assigned role, observations, communications, decisions and how changing circumstances affected them.

Instructor Observation Criteria

Objective Understanding Understands what the operation is intended to achieve.
Information Handling Separates confirmed, reported and unknown information.
Role Discipline Understands and performs the assigned responsibility.
Communication Shares relevant information clearly.
Team Awareness Remains aware of colleagues and wider operations.
Person Assessment Avoids automatically treating unknown persons as threats.
Resource Awareness Understands the purpose of specialist capabilities.
Reassessment Recognises when circumstances change.
Adaptability Changes the response when the existing plan becomes unsuitable.
Accountability Explains decisions clearly during debrief.

Immediate Stop Criteria

Stop or reset the exercise if a student:

  • Ignores a trainer safety command.
  • Continues despite being confused about the scenario or role.
  • Acts outside the agreed Code44 training boundary.
  • Repeatedly separates from the team without communication.
  • Fails to communicate significant changes.
  • Repeatedly ignores civilians or other officers.
  • Uses specialist resources contrary to trainer direction.
  • Continues despite a serious server or communication failure.
  • Displays repeated poor firearms discipline.
  • Refuses trainer direction.

Module Outcomes

Pass

The student demonstrates satisfactory building-operation awareness, teamwork, communication, role discipline, reassessment and accountability.

Pass with Development Points

The student may progress but has limited development areas to continue working on during later complex scenarios.

Remedial

Additional trainer input or controlled building scenarios are required before progression.

Incomplete

Required pre-learning, practical work or assessment remains outstanding.

Unsuccessful

The student has not demonstrated the required standard after appropriate instruction, feedback and reassessment.

Common Student Errors

  • Rushing to appear confident.
  • Failing to understand the objective.
  • Ignoring important unknowns.
  • Separating unnecessarily from the team.
  • Talking constantly instead of communicating relevant information.
  • Assuming every unknown person is the suspect.
  • Losing awareness of other officers.
  • Requesting specialist resources without a clear reason.
  • Continuing the original plan after circumstances change.
  • Failing to communicate a server or radio problem.
  • Providing a poor handover.

Common Trainer Errors

  • Making the first scenario unnecessarily complex.
  • Failing to give students a clear objective.
  • Assessing speed rather than coordination.
  • Allowing one student to dominate the team.
  • Providing every piece of information without requiring students to think.
  • Failing to introduce civilians or unknown persons.
  • Using specialist equipment without explaining why.
  • Failing to change information during scenarios.
  • Not assessing students individually.
  • Giving away the preferred answer before the debrief.
  • Failing to record development points.

Closing Script

Suggested wording

“That concludes Module 10.

Building operations are not about racing through rooms or memorising one fixed solution.

They rely on communication, awareness, role discipline and understanding what the team is trying to achieve.

The strongest performance is controlled, adaptable and accountable.

Module 11 will bring these skills together during complex armed incidents involving multiple units and specialist capabilities.”
