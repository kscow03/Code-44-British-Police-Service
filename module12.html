Module 12

Consolidation & Scenario Development

ARU Academy — Student Learning & Final Preparation

Final Development Module

Module Overview

Module 12 is the final development stage of the ARU Academy before the Final Assessment.

Very little new theory is introduced within this module.

Instead, students will consolidate the knowledge, professional standards, communication skills, decision-making and practical abilities developed throughout Modules 1–11.

Training will focus heavily on scenarios, trainer feedback and identifying any remaining development areas before the student progresses to final assessment.

The Purpose of Module 12

Module 12 is not designed to catch students out.

It exists to identify weaknesses while there is still time to correct them.

Students should use this stage to demonstrate consistent performance, ask questions, seek clarification and apply feedback before entering the final assessment stage.

1. Learning Objectives

Consistency Demonstrate Academy standards consistently rather than only during individual exercises.
Integration Combine knowledge from all previous modules during realistic scenarios.
Decision-Making Make proportionate and explainable decisions as circumstances change.
Communication Communicate effectively with crew members, other units and command.
Adaptability Recognise when a plan is no longer suitable and adjust appropriately.
Self-Development Identify personal strengths and development areas before final assessment.

2. Academy Standards

At this stage of the Academy, trainers will expect students to demonstrate the standards taught throughout the entire course without requiring constant reminders.

Professionalism Remain professional, respectful and accountable throughout training.
Integrity Be honest about mistakes, uncertainty and performance.
Communication Pass relevant information clearly and listen to others.
Teamwork Work as part of the wider ARU team rather than operating independently.
Decision-Making Base decisions on the information and circumstances available at the time.
Reassessment Recognise when changing circumstances require the response to change.
Safety Maintain firearms, vehicle and equipment discipline.
Accountability Be capable of explaining actions and decisions afterwards.

Do Not Memorise Scenarios

Students should not attempt to memorise previous exercises and repeat the same response whenever a similar scenario appears.

Different information, behaviour, locations and risks may require different decisions.

Trainers will deliberately vary scenarios to assess understanding rather than memorisation.

4. Decision-Making

Throughout Module 12, students should demonstrate structured and proportionate decision-making.

Students should continually consider:

  • What information do I currently have?
  • What remains uncertain?
  • What threat currently exists?
  • Who is at risk?
  • What am I trying to achieve?
  • What options are available?
  • Does my current plan remain appropriate?
  • What has changed since my previous assessment?
Final Preparation Principle Trainers are not looking for perfect hindsight. They are looking for reasonable and explainable decisions based on what you knew at the time.

5. Communication

By Module 12, students should communicate without trainers constantly prompting them.

Relevant information should be communicated when:

  • The threat changes.
  • A subject is located or moves.
  • Weapon information changes.
  • A casualty occurs.
  • A vehicle becomes involved.
  • A containment changes or fails.
  • A significant civilian risk appears.
  • A task cannot be completed.
  • A specialist resource is required.
  • The existing plan is no longer suitable.

Students should balance passing important information with maintaining disciplined radio traffic.

6. Teamwork

ARU operations require officers to work effectively as part of a team.

Students should demonstrate:

  • Listening to crew members.
  • Sharing information.
  • Supporting the assigned plan.
  • Understanding their role.
  • Speaking up when important information is identified.
  • Requesting help where required.
  • Accepting direction.
  • Not attempting to dominate every scenario.
Strong Team Performance A good ARU officer makes the team more effective, not just themselves.

7. Information Handling

Students should consistently distinguish between:

  • Reported information.
  • Confirmed information.
  • Personal observations.
  • Assumptions.
  • Important unknowns.

Students should not create certainty where it does not exist.

Complex incidents may contain conflicting information and trainers will expect students to recognise this.

8. Adaptability

Students should demonstrate that they can change their response when circumstances materially change.

Examples include:

  • A weapon being confirmed.
  • A reported weapon being disproved.
  • A subject becoming compliant.
  • A subject becoming more threatening.
  • An additional subject being identified.
  • A civilian or hostage appearing.
  • A vehicle becoming involved.
  • A casualty occurring.
  • A specialist resource becoming available.
  • A server mechanic failing.
  • Command changing the operational objective.
Do Not Force an Outdated Plan A plan should be changed, paused or abandoned when it no longer matches the circumstances.

9. Scenario Development

Module 12 practical exercises may draw from any previous Academy module.

Students may encounter scenarios involving:

  • Initial ARV deployment.
  • Vehicle incidents.
  • Containment.
  • Search operations.
  • High-risk warrants.
  • Building operations.
  • Multiple subjects.
  • Multiple locations.
  • NPAS integration.
  • K9 integration.
  • Casualties.
  • Conflicting intelligence.
  • Specialist resources.
  • Changing operational priorities.

Trainers may combine several elements into one scenario.

Scenario Difficulty

Students are not expected to perform every scenario perfectly.

Trainers may deliberately introduce uncertainty or unexpected changes to assess whether students remain controlled and adaptable.

Making an error does not automatically mean failure.

Failing to recognise, communicate or learn from repeated errors is more significant.

10. Feedback & Development

Feedback is particularly important during Module 12.

Students should:

  • Listen to trainer feedback.
  • Ask questions if feedback is unclear.
  • Understand why a development point has been identified.
  • Apply feedback during later exercises.
  • Be honest about areas they found difficult.
  • Avoid becoming defensive when mistakes are discussed.
Trainer Feedback Is Preparation Module 12 is the opportunity to fix issues before they appear during final assessment.

11. Identifying Development Areas

Students should reflect on their own performance.

Possible development areas may include:

  • Radio communication.
  • Confidence.
  • Overconfidence.
  • Information handling.
  • Team awareness.
  • Decision-making.
  • Reassessment.
  • Vehicle coordination.
  • Building-operation awareness.
  • Role discipline.
  • Specialist-resource awareness.
  • Post-incident handovers.

Identifying a weakness is not a failure.

The purpose is to improve it before qualification.

Repeated Development Points

Trainers may require additional training if the same issue repeatedly appears despite previous feedback.

Students should demonstrate that they are capable of taking feedback and applying it during later exercises.

12. Final Assessment Readiness

Before progression to Module 13, students should demonstrate satisfactory performance across the core Academy standards.

Professionalism Consistent professional conduct.
Knowledge Satisfactory understanding of Academy learning.
Safety Reliable firearms, vehicle and equipment discipline.
Communication Clear and relevant communication.
Teamwork Effective contribution to ARU team performance.
Decision-Making Proportionate and explainable decisions.
Reassessment Recognition and response to changing circumstances.
Accountability Ability to explain actions honestly after an incident.

Progression to Final Assessment

Completion of Module 12 does not automatically guarantee immediate progression to Module 13.

Trainers may recommend additional development where a student has an outstanding issue that should be addressed before final assessment.

This is intended to give the student the best opportunity to demonstrate the required standard when formally assessed.

13. Final Revision

Before final assessment, students should revisit any Academy module they are unsure about.

Students should be comfortable explaining:

  • The professional standards expected within ARU.
  • How accountability applies to armed policing.
  • The importance of continuous reassessment.
  • The difference between reported and confirmed information.
  • ARU equipment, firearms and PPE.
  • Firearms safety principles.
  • ARV crew responsibilities.
  • Armed vehicle incident principles.
  • Containment and search principles.
  • Building-operation principles.
  • How ARU works with Response, Roads Policing, NPAS and K9.
  • How casualties may affect an operation.
  • Why specialist capabilities should only be used for a clear purpose.
  • How changing circumstances should affect operational decisions.
  • What information should be included within a post-incident handover.

What Good Looks Like

A student ready for final assessment does not need to know everything instantly.

They should be able to:

  • Remain calm.
  • Think.
  • Communicate.
  • Work with others.
  • Ask for information.
  • Recognise risk.
  • Make a decision.
  • Change that decision when necessary.
  • Accept feedback.
  • Explain what they did afterwards.

Module 12 Completion

Module 12 is complete when the student has:

  • Reviewed required Academy learning.
  • Completed consolidation scenarios.
  • Demonstrated consistent professional standards.
  • Demonstrated satisfactory communication.
  • Demonstrated effective teamwork.
  • Demonstrated accountable decision-making.
  • Demonstrated continuous reassessment.
  • Completed identified remedial training where required.
  • Received and applied trainer feedback.
  • Been recommended as ready for final assessment.

Final Assessment & Qualification

Module 13 is the final stage of the ARU Academy.

Students will be assessed across knowledge, professionalism, practical performance, communication, decision-making, teamwork and accountability.

The assessment is designed to determine whether the student can perform consistently at the standard expected of a qualified Code44 ARU officer.
