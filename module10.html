Module 10

Building Operations & CQB

ARU Academy — Student Learning & Reference

Tactical Operations

Module Overview

Module 10 introduces students to the principles of operating as part of an armed team within buildings and other confined environments.

The focus of this module is communication, team awareness, role discipline, controlled movement, information handling, threat reassessment and safe integration with specialist resources.

This page is designed as pre-learning and as a reference officers can return to later.

Exact entry techniques, room-clearing patterns, weapon manipulation, physical positioning and real-world CQB methods are taught only through approved Code44 practical training.

1. Learning Objectives

Team Operations Understand that building operations depend on coordinated team activity.
Communication Understand the importance of clear information sharing within confined environments.
Awareness Maintain awareness of team members, civilians, subjects and changing risks.
Role Discipline Understand and perform the responsibility assigned within the team.
Reassessment Recognise that information and threat can change as the operation develops.
Accountability Explain decisions and actions taken during a building operation.

2. Building Operations

Buildings create additional complexity during armed incidents.

Officers may have limited information regarding who is inside, where subjects are located and whether members of the public remain within the location.

Building operations may involve:

  • Searching for an armed subject.
  • Locating vulnerable persons.
  • Supporting a high-risk warrant.
  • Responding to an immediate threat.
  • Clearing a location after an incident.
  • Supporting specialist operations.
Key Principle A building operation is not simply about getting inside. The objective is to manage the threat while maintaining control, communication and team awareness.

3. CQB Principles

Close-quarters operations involve officers working within confined environments where situations can develop quickly.

Students should understand the following core principles:

  • Work as a team rather than as individuals.
  • Understand the operational objective.
  • Know your assigned responsibility.
  • Maintain awareness of other officers.
  • Communicate relevant changes.
  • Do not rush simply to appear confident.
  • Continually reassess the threat.
  • Recognise civilians and uninvolved persons.
  • Follow trainer or command direction.
Important This module explains Code44 principles only. Practical CQB procedure, exact positioning and entry technique must be learned from an approved trainer.

4. Information Before Entry

Before entering or searching a building, students should understand as much relevant information as possible.

This may include:

  • The operational objective.
  • Subject identity and description.
  • Known or suspected weapons.
  • Number of subjects.
  • Known civilians or occupants.
  • Vulnerable persons or hostages.
  • Known location information.
  • Information regarding entrances or access.
  • Relevant intelligence.
  • Available specialist resources.
  • Medical considerations.
  • Important unknowns.

Students must distinguish between information that is confirmed, reported or unknown.

Never Create Certainty Where It Does Not Exist

If officers do not know how many subjects are inside, they should not behave as though the number is confirmed.

If a firearm has only been reported, officers should understand the difference between the report and personal confirmation.

Unknown information should remain part of the risk assessment.

5. Team Roles

Exact team structures may change depending on the scenario, number of available officers and operational objective.

Team Lead Supports coordination and ensures the team understands the immediate objective.
Team Member Performs the assigned task while maintaining awareness and communication.
Shield Capability May provide additional protection where authorised and suitable.
Breaching Capability May assist with authorised access where the operational plan requires it.
Containment May remain outside or around the location to support wider control.
Firearms Command Provides tactical oversight and operational direction where required.

Students should understand their own responsibility while remaining aware of the wider team.

6. Communication

Communication inside buildings should be relevant, controlled and timely.

Useful information may include:

  • Subject sightings.
  • Civilians located.
  • New weapon information.
  • Changes in subject behaviour.
  • Team member movement.
  • Changes to the operational objective.
  • Medical emergencies.
  • Loss of communication or technical issues.
  • Areas requiring further attention.
  • Information requiring command awareness.

Officers should avoid unnecessary communication that prevents important information from being heard.

7. Team Movement

Movement during building operations should be controlled and coordinated.

Students should understand that:

  • The team should maintain a shared understanding of the immediate objective.
  • Officers should remain aware of where other team members are.
  • Individuals should not unnecessarily separate from the team.
  • Significant movement or changes should be communicated.
  • Officers should avoid rushing ahead of the rest of the team.
  • Confusion should be communicated rather than hidden.
  • Trainer safety commands must be followed immediately.
Controlled Does Not Mean Slow The aim is coordinated and deliberate performance rather than unnecessary speed.

8. Awareness & Cross-Team Safety

Students must maintain awareness of more than the suspected threat.

Consider:

  • Where are other ARU officers?
  • Are response officers or other units nearby?
  • Have civilians entered the area?
  • Has the subject moved?
  • Has the team's role changed?
  • Has another team entered the location?
  • Has radio information changed?
  • Is the current plan still appropriate?

Tunnel vision is a major development point trainers will assess during practical CQB scenarios.

9. Civilians & Unknown Persons

Not every person located inside a building should automatically be treated as the armed subject.

Officers may encounter:

  • Members of the public.
  • Employees.
  • Residents.
  • Victims.
  • Witnesses.
  • Hostages.
  • Additional suspects.
  • Persons whose involvement is initially unknown.

Students should assess behaviour and available information rather than applying assumptions automatically.

10. Ballistic Shield

ARU has access to a ballistic shield capability.

The shield may be incorporated into selected Code44 scenarios where its use supports the operational objective.

Students should understand that:

  • The shield is a specialist resource.
  • Its use should support the operational objective.
  • The presence of a shield does not remove the need for communication.
  • The wider team must understand how the shield is being incorporated.
  • Students should follow trainer direction during shield exercises.

Detailed shield positioning and movement are taught practically.

11. Breaching Capability

Code44 ARU has access to the Remington shotgun as a specialist breaching capability.

It is not used as the standard operational shotgun for routine armed incidents.

During building operations, breaching may be considered where authorised access is required and the operational plan supports the capability.

Students should understand:

  • Breaching is a specialist responsibility.
  • The need for access should be established before use.
  • The team should understand when breaching is part of the plan.
  • Communication is required before the operation progresses.
  • Server-specific breaching mechanics are taught practically.
Specialist Capability Module 10 does not independently qualify a student as a breaching specialist.

12. Specialist Integration

Building operations may involve additional specialist capabilities.

K9 May support searches, tracking or other authorised tasks.
NPAS May provide external observation and situational awareness.
Ballistic Shield May provide additional protection within selected operations.
Breaching May provide authorised access capability.
Medical Capability Supports immediate casualty management where required.
Response Officers May support cordons, evacuation, witnesses and wider scene management.
Resource Selection Specialist resources should be requested because they solve an operational problem, not simply because they are available.

13. Changing Circumstances

Building operations require continuous reassessment.

The plan may need to change if:

  • The subject moves location.
  • An additional subject is identified.
  • A civilian or hostage is located.
  • A weapon is confirmed or disproved.
  • The subject begins complying.
  • The subject becomes more threatening.
  • A team member loses communication.
  • A server mechanic fails.
  • A casualty occurs.
  • Command changes the objective.
  • Information indicates the current plan is no longer suitable.
Key Principle Do not continue a plan simply because the team has already started it. New information should result in new assessment.

Immediate Threat vs Planned Search

Not every building operation has the same urgency.

A scenario involving an immediate ongoing threat may require a very different response from a controlled search where containment has already been established.

Students should understand the difference between:

  • An immediate threat requiring urgent action within the scenario.
  • A controlled search where time is available.
  • A planned warrant operation.
  • A post-incident building search.

The operational response should reflect the actual circumstances rather than applying one fixed CQB response to everything.

14. Resolution & Handover

Once the immediate threat is controlled, officers should transition into the next stage of the incident.

This may include:

  • Confirming subject status.
  • Confirming whether all subjects are accounted for.
  • Locating civilians or vulnerable persons.
  • Identifying medical requirements.
  • Updating firearms command and Force Control.
  • Protecting the scene.
  • Identifying witnesses.
  • Handing over to investigators or response officers.
  • Accounting for ARU equipment.

Post-Operation Accountability

Students should be able to explain:

  • What information was available.
  • What remained uncertain.
  • What threat they believed existed.
  • What their assigned role was.
  • What they personally observed.
  • What they communicated.
  • Why they made particular decisions.
  • What changed during the operation.
  • How their response changed.

Honest reflection is an important part of ARU development.

15. Practical Training

Module 10 is primarily a practical training module.

Students will apply the principles within controlled Code44 scenarios.

Practical training may include:

  • Team briefing exercises.
  • Building familiarisation.
  • Team communication exercises.
  • Controlled search scenarios.
  • Multiple-room environments.
  • Civilians within the location.
  • Multiple subjects.
  • Changing weapon information.
  • Ballistic shield familiarisation.
  • Breaching-capability familiarisation.
  • K9 integration.
  • Casualty scenarios.
  • Changing operational objectives.
  • Post-operation handover.

Scenarios will vary. Students should not attempt to memorise a single “correct” solution.

What Trainers Will Assess

Students should demonstrate:

  • Professional preparation.
  • Understanding of the operational objective.
  • Controlled team behaviour.
  • Communication.
  • Awareness of other officers.
  • Recognition of civilians and unknown persons.
  • Information handling.
  • Threat reassessment.
  • Ability to follow direction.
  • Appropriate use of specialist resources.
  • Ability to recognise when the plan should change.
  • Accountability during debrief.

16. Module 10 Key Revision Points

  • Building operations are team operations.
  • The operational objective should be understood before action begins.
  • Students must distinguish confirmed information from assumptions.
  • Each officer should understand their assigned responsibility.
  • Communication should remain clear and relevant.
  • Controlled movement is more important than unnecessary speed.
  • Maintain awareness of other officers and units.
  • Not every person inside a building is automatically a suspect.
  • The ballistic shield is a specialist capability.
  • The Remington is used as a specialist breaching platform.
  • Specialist resources should support the operational objective.
  • Immediate threats and planned searches are not the same.
  • The plan must change when circumstances materially change.
  • Resolution includes medical care, scene management and handover.
  • Officers must be capable of explaining their decisions afterwards.

Student Reminder

This student module is intended to support Code44 roleplay training and provide a reference after qualification.

It does not independently qualify a student to conduct or instruct practical CQB, breaching or specialist building-entry procedures.

Practical techniques must be demonstrated, practised and assessed by an approved ARU trainer using the current Code44 server mechanics.
