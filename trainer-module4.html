Trainer Module 4 — Equipment, Vehicles and PPE

Instructor Lesson Plan

ARU Academy • Cohort 001

Trainer-Only Resource

Lesson Overview

Module 4 introduces students to the equipment, vehicles, protective equipment and firearms capabilities used by Code44 ARU.

This lesson focuses on identification, role, storage, accountability and equipment checks. It does not provide practical instruction in operating or firing firearms.

Students should leave the session understanding that specialist equipment is entrusted to them and must remain secure, accounted for and ready for operational use.

Lesson Module 4 — Equipment, Vehicles and PPE
Recommended Duration 90 minutes
Current Group Two ARU students
Delivery Style Briefing, demonstration and vehicle walkthrough
Assessment Identification exercise and knowledge check
Pass Standard Satisfactory practical demonstration and 80% quiz score

Trainer Safety Boundary

Module 4 is a familiarisation lesson only.

Trainers must not use this lesson to deliver firearms handling, loading, firing, breaching or tactical employment instruction.

Practical firearms instruction begins during the approved firearms safety and handling phase of the Academy.

Trainer Preparation

Before delivering the session, the trainer should:

  • Confirm both students completed Module 3
  • Confirm both students have read Module 4
  • Prepare a marked BMW X7
  • Prepare an unmarked BMW X7 where available
  • Confirm the vehicles are parked in a suitable training location
  • Confirm representative PPE is available
  • Confirm representative medical equipment is available through roleplay
  • Confirm firearms remain secured unless required for authorised display
  • Prepare the equipment-identification exercise
  • Prepare the pre-deployment check exercise
  • Prepare the fault-reporting scenario
  • Prepare the Module 4 knowledge check
  • Prepare an attendance record
  • Confirm the trainer assessment form is available

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, each student should be able to:

  • Identify the main equipment used by Code44 ARU.
  • Explain the purpose of standard ARU PPE.
  • Identify the SIG P226 personal sidearm.
  • Identify the SIG MCX primary carbine.
  • Identify the SIG 716 precision capability.
  • Explain the dedicated breaching role of the Remington shotgun.
  • Identify the marked and unmarked BMW X7.
  • Explain where long firearms must be stored.
  • Identify the sidearm exception to the vehicle-storage rule.
  • Complete a basic pre-deployment equipment check.
  • Explain how missing or damaged equipment should be reported.
  • Understand personal responsibility for issued equipment.

Opening Script

Suggested wording

“Module 4 introduces the equipment, protective systems and vehicles used by Code44 ARU.

Today is not a firearms handling lesson. You are learning what each capability is, why it exists, where it is stored and what responsibility you accept when it is entrusted to you.

Every item must remain secure, accounted for and ready for the next officer who may depend upon it.”

Stage 1 — Equipment Accountability

Explain that specialist equipment is issued for an operational purpose and must not be treated as a status symbol.

Students should understand that equipment must be:

  • Appropriate for the authorised task.
  • Present and accounted for.
  • Checked before deployment.
  • Stored securely when not required.
  • Used only by trained and authorised personnel.
  • Returned to the correct location.
  • Checked for faults or damage.
  • Reported immediately if missing.
Trainer Prompt Why is failing to report a missing item more serious than simply discovering that something is missing?

Expected learning points

  • The missing equipment may be required during an emergency.
  • Another crew may assume the item is available.
  • Failure to report it prevents the issue from being resolved.
  • Concealment may become a professional-standards matter.

Discussion — Equipment as a Responsibility

Ask both students:

Question What responsibilities do you accept when you remove an item from an ARV or equipment store?

Expected learning points

  • Know what the item is for.
  • Confirm authority to use it.
  • Keep it secure.
  • Avoid unauthorised use.
  • Report faults or damage.
  • Return it to the correct place.
  • Confirm it is accounted for afterwards.

Stage 2 — Personal Protective Equipment

Introduce each item individually.

Plate Carrier Protective and load-bearing equipment used to carry ballistic protection and authorised operational items.
Ballistic Plates Protective inserts fitted within the plate carrier.
Ballistic Helmet Protective head equipment used during authorised training and deployments.
Eye Protection Protective equipment used during relevant training and operational activity.
Ear Protection Protective equipment required during firearms training and high-noise activity.
Night-Vision Equipment Specialist equipment used during authorised low-light operations and training.
Gas Mask Specialist protective equipment used where authorised by the operational plan.
Ballistic Shield Specialist protective capability used only by trained officers when appropriate.

PPE Identification Exercise

Present or describe each item individually and ask each student to explain:

  • What the item is.
  • Its general purpose.
  • Whether it is personal or specialist equipment.
  • When trainer or command authority may be required.
  • What should happen if it is damaged.

Do not assess detailed tactical employment during this exercise.

Stage 3 — Medical Capability

Explain that Code44 ARU roleplays a comprehensive trauma and emergency medical capability.

The capability includes:

  • Individual first-aid equipment
  • Tourniquets
  • Chest seals
  • Haemostatic dressings
  • Pressure dressings
  • Trauma shears
  • Airway equipment
  • Emergency blankets
  • Oxygen equipment
  • AED
  • Medical backpack or trauma bag

Make clear that detailed casualty-care instruction is delivered during a later tactical medical module.

Key teaching point Used medical equipment must be identified, reported and replaced before the ARV is returned to service.

Stage 4 — Firearms Role Familiarisation

Introduce the role of each firearm without teaching practical handling.

Capability Role Storage Requirement
SIG Sauer P226 Personal sidearm carried by trained and authorised ARU officers. The personal sidearm is the exception to the rear vehicle firearms-storage requirement.
SIG MCX Primary operational carbine used during authorised ARU deployments. Secured within the designated rear vehicle firearms storage when not authorised for deployment.
SIG 716 Specialist precision-rifle capability used during authorised roles. Secured within the rear vehicle firearms storage until authorised.
Remington Shotgun Specialist breaching platform. It is not the routine primary firearm for general ARU deployment. Secured within the rear vehicle firearms storage unless authorised for a breaching role.

Firearms Role Identification Exercise

Name or display each capability and ask each student to identify:

  • The name of the capability.
  • Its role within Code44 ARU.
  • Whether it is personal, primary or specialist equipment.
  • Where it should be stored when not deployed.
  • Whether additional authority may be required.

Expected answers

  • P226 — personal sidearm.
  • MCX — primary operational carbine.
  • SIG 716 — specialist precision capability.
  • Remington — specialist breaching platform only.

Remington Breaching Platform

Trainers must be clear that the Remington is used as a dedicated breaching tool.

It is not to be presented as the standard primary firearm for routine deployments or general room-search activity.

Detailed breaching instruction is delivered separately to appropriately trained and authorised personnel.

Stage 5 — Less-Lethal and Supporting Equipment

Taser Available to trained and authorised officers under current Code44 policy and server rules.
PAVA Authorised officer-safety equipment requiring sound judgement and accountability.
Flashbangs Specialist capability available where supported by the server script and authorised for use.

Remind students that Code44 ARU does not currently have a beanbag shotgun capability.

Stage 6 — ARU Vehicle Familiarisation

Code44 ARU currently operates:

  • Marked BMW X7
  • Unmarked BMW X7

Explain that both variants are subject to the same security, accountability and equipment-check standards.

Crewing

ARVs are normally double-crewed by trained ARU officers.

Both crew members share responsibility for:

  • Understanding the vehicle's operational status.
  • Confirming firearms are secured.
  • Confirming required PPE is present.
  • Confirming medical equipment is available.
  • Checking communications equipment.
  • Reporting faults or shortages.
  • Completing end-of-tour checks.

Marked and Unmarked X7 Walkthrough

Walk the students around both vehicles where available.

Ask them to identify:

  • The marked and unmarked vehicle.
  • Driver and passenger positions.
  • The rear equipment area.
  • The designated firearms-storage area.
  • The medical-equipment area.
  • The location of specialist PPE.
  • Any communications or supporting equipment.

The exact internal arrangement may vary depending upon current server vehicle configuration. Trainers should teach the current approved layout.

Stage 7 — Firearms Storage

Teach the following Code44 rule clearly:

Vehicle Firearms Rule All firearms other than the officer's personal P226 sidearm must remain secured in the rear firearms-storage area of the ARV unless deployment has been authorised.

This includes:

  • SIG MCX carbines
  • SIG 716 precision rifles
  • Remington breaching shotguns

Students must understand:

  • Long firearms must not be left unsecured.
  • Unauthorised removal must be reported.
  • Every firearm must be accounted for before and after deployment.
  • Returned firearms must be secured correctly.
  • The vehicle must not be left with unsecured firearms.

Pre-Deployment Check Exercise

Place both students beside an ARV and ask them to conduct a verbal pre-deployment equipment check.

They should confirm:

  • The vehicle appears serviceable
  • The marked or unmarked configuration is understood
  • Required firearms are present
  • Long firearms are secured in the rear
  • Personal sidearms are accounted for
  • Required PPE is present
  • The medical kit is present
  • The ballistic shield is present where required
  • Night-vision equipment is present where authorised
  • Communications equipment is available
  • Faults or shortages have been reported

Assess the students' process and communication rather than speed.

Scenario One — Missing Medical Equipment

During the pre-deployment check, a student notices that part of the trauma kit is missing.

Ask:

  • What should the student do?
  • Can the issue simply be ignored?
  • Who should be informed?
  • What information should be provided?
  • Who decides whether the vehicle remains deployable?

Expected learning points

  • The shortage must be reported promptly.
  • The missing item should be identified clearly.
  • The appropriate trainer or supervisor should be informed.
  • The issue should be resolved or formally accepted.
  • The student must not conceal the shortage.

Scenario Two — Unsecured Long Firearm

At the end of a training exercise, a student notices that an MCX has been placed in the rear of the ARV but has not been secured properly.

Ask:

  • What immediate action is required?
  • Should the vehicle be left unattended?
  • Who needs to be informed?
  • What should be checked afterwards?
  • Why is this an accountability issue?

Expected learning points

  • The firearm must be secured immediately by authorised personnel.
  • The vehicle must not be left with an unsecured firearm.
  • The issue should be reported.
  • All other firearms should also be checked.
  • The circumstances should be reviewed to prevent repetition.

Scenario Three — Damaged PPE

A student discovers visible damage to a ballistic helmet before training.

Ask:

  • Should the helmet continue to be used?
  • What should the student do?
  • Who should be informed?
  • What alternative equipment may be required?
  • Why must the fault be recorded?

Expected learning points

  • The damage must not be ignored.
  • The trainer or supervisor should be informed.
  • A replacement should be considered.
  • The damaged item should be removed from normal use where appropriate.
  • The fault should be documented.

Stage 8 — End-of-Tour Checks

Students should confirm:

  • All deployed firearms have been returned
  • All long firearms are secured
  • No equipment remains at the training or incident location
  • Used medical items have been identified
  • Damaged equipment has been reported
  • Missing equipment has been reported
  • The vehicle has been checked for damage
  • The vehicle is ready for the next crew
  • Relevant records have been completed

Review Questions

  1. What responsibility does an officer accept when issued specialist equipment?
  2. What PPE is available to Code44 ARU?
  3. What is the role of the SIG P226?
  4. What is the role of the SIG MCX?
  5. What is the role of the SIG 716?
  6. What is the Remington shotgun used for?
  7. Where must long firearms be stored when not deployed?
  8. What is the exception to the rear firearms-storage rule?
  9. What ARVs does Code44 operate?
  10. How are ARVs normally crewed?
  11. What should happen when equipment is missing or damaged?
  12. What must happen to used medical equipment?
  13. Does Module 4 authorise practical firearms handling?

Expected Answers

  1. Keep it secure, use it appropriately, report faults and return it correctly.
  2. Plate carrier, ballistic plates, helmet, eye and ear protection, NVG, gas mask and ballistic shield.
  3. Personal sidearm carried by trained and authorised ARU officers.
  4. Primary operational carbine.
  5. Specialist precision-rifle capability.
  6. Specialist breaching platform only.
  7. Within the designated rear firearms-storage area of the ARV.
  8. The officer's personal P226 sidearm.
  9. Marked and unmarked BMW X7.
  10. Normally double-crewed by trained ARU officers.
  11. Report the issue promptly and provide clear details.
  12. It must be identified, reported and replaced.
  13. No. Module 4 is familiarisation only.

Module Outcomes

Pass

The student identifies the principal equipment, understands storage requirements and completes the vehicle check satisfactorily.

Pass with Development Points

The student may progress but has limited areas requiring further review.

Remedial

The student requires further familiarisation, questioning or another vehicle-check exercise.

Incomplete

Required attendance, practical familiarisation or assessment remains outstanding.

Unsuccessful

The student has not met the required standard after appropriate support.

Common Student Errors

  • Treating equipment as an entitlement rather than a responsibility.
  • Confusing the role of the MCX and SIG 716.
  • Describing the Remington as a routine primary firearm.
  • Forgetting that long firearms must remain secured in the rear.
  • Failing to identify the P226 sidearm exception.
  • Completing checks too quickly and missing items.
  • Failing to report missing or damaged equipment.
  • Assuming the other crew member completed the check.

Common Trainer Errors

  • Turning familiarisation into practical firearms handling.
  • Teaching detailed tactical use too early.
  • Failing to explain equipment accountability.
  • Skipping the unmarked vehicle walkthrough.
  • Allowing students to observe without participating.
  • Failing to assess both students individually.
  • Not correcting the role of the Remington shotgun.
  • Failing to record equipment-identification weaknesses.

Closing Script

Module 5 begins the firearms safety and handling phase. Until that lesson is delivered, you are not authorised by this module to conduct practical firearms handling.”
