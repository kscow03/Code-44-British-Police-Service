Trainer Module 5 — Firearms Safety and Handling

Instructor Lesson Plan

ARU Academy • Cohort 001

Trainer-Only Resource

Lesson Overview

Module 5 begins the firearms-safety phase of the ARU Academy.

This session teaches safe behaviour, trainer commands, environmental awareness, accountability and the professional standards expected during simulated firearms training.

The emphasis is on disciplined conduct and safe decision-making. This lesson must not be used to teach real-world firing, loading, breaching or weapon-operation procedures.

Lesson Module 5 — Firearms Safety and Handling
Recommended Duration 90 minutes
Current Group Two ARU students
Delivery Style Briefing, demonstration and supervised simulation
Assessment Safety observation and knowledge check
Pass Standard Safe conduct, satisfactory participation and 80% quiz score

Mandatory Trainer Boundary

This lesson is designed for Code44 FiveM roleplay.

Trainers must not use this page as real-world weapons instruction.

The session must remain focused on simulation safety, trainer commands, professional conduct, storage, accountability and scenario control.

Any exercise must stop immediately if a student becomes confused, ignores a safety command or acts outside the agreed roleplay boundaries.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the lesson, each student should be able to:

  • Explain why firearms safety is everyone's responsibility.
  • Respond immediately to trainer safety commands.
  • Maintain awareness of other officers and roleplayers.
  • Explain the importance of safe direction.
  • Demonstrate appropriate trigger discipline in simulation.
  • Explain the need for positive identification.
  • Recognise when an exercise must be stopped.
  • Communicate clearly when confused or uncertain.
  • Identify the role of each Code44 firearm.
  • Explain the ARV storage and accountability requirements.
  • Respond professionally to correction.
  • Demonstrate safe conduct during supervised roleplay.

Opening Script

Suggested wording

“Module 5 begins the firearms-safety phase of the Academy.

This lesson is about discipline, awareness, communication and responding correctly to trainer commands.

It is not about speed, showing off or trying to look experienced.

If you are unsure at any point, stop and ask. A scenario can be restarted, but unsafe behaviour will always take priority over completing the exercise.”

Stage 1 — Shared Safety Responsibility

Explain that safety is not solely the responsibility of the instructor.

Every student must:

  • Listen fully to the briefing.
  • Follow trainer commands immediately.
  • Maintain awareness of other people.
  • Speak up when something appears unsafe.
  • Stop when confused.
  • Report mistakes honestly.
  • Avoid rushing or showing off.
  • Treat simulated training seriously.
  • Accept correction professionally.
Trainer Prompt If you notice another student has misunderstood an instruction, should you remain silent because the trainer is in charge?

Expected learning points

  • Safety is shared.
  • Students should raise concerns promptly.
  • Professional challenge is acceptable when safety is involved.
  • The trainer retains control of the exercise.

Stage 2 — Core Safety Principles

Treat the Exercise Seriously Simulation does not make careless behaviour acceptable.
Maintain Safe Direction Remain aware of where the firearm is directed and who is nearby.
Trigger Discipline Avoid unnecessary trigger contact and follow trainer direction.
Positive Identification Confirm who and what is being assessed before making a simulated decision.
Follow Commands Stop, hold and safety instructions must be obeyed immediately.
Stop When Unsure Clarification is safer than guessing.
Maintain Control Do not rush or allow excitement to replace discipline.
Report Errors Honest reporting allows mistakes to be corrected.

Discussion — What Stops the Exercise?

Ask each student to identify circumstances that should cause them to stop.

Strong answers may include:

  • They do not understand the instruction.
  • Another officer moves unexpectedly.
  • A roleplayer enters the wrong area.
  • The server script behaves unexpectedly.
  • An item appears faulty.
  • A trainer gives a stop command.
  • The student loses awareness of their assigned role.
  • The student believes a safety concern exists.

Stage 3 — Trainer and Safety Commands

The exact wording may vary, but students must understand the following:

Command Expected Student Response
Stop Immediately stop the current action and await further instruction.
Hold Position Remain in place and avoid unnecessary movement.
Safe Return to the trainer-directed safe condition for the simulation.
Stand Down Recognise that the current phase or exercise has ended.
Do Not Continue Await clarification before taking further action.
Safety Stop Stop immediately because an actual or potential concern has arisen.

Command Response Drill

Place both students within the controlled training area.

Give each command individually and assess whether the students:

  • Respond immediately.
  • Stop unnecessary movement.
  • Avoid talking over the trainer.
  • Wait for further instruction.
  • Remain calm and professional.

Repeat any command that is not understood consistently.

Stage 4 — Safe Direction and Awareness

Explain that a safe direction depends upon the environment and the positions of everyone involved.

Students must remain aware of:

  • Other officers.
  • Trainers.
  • Roleplayers.
  • Vehicles.
  • Doorways and access routes.
  • Members of the public within the scenario.
  • Changes in team position.
  • The wider area around the intended subject.
Key teaching point Looking in a new direction does not remove responsibility for maintaining awareness of the simulated firearm and surrounding officers.

Environmental Awareness Exercise

Place the students in a simple controlled scene.

Ask each student to identify:

  • Where the trainer is positioned.
  • Where the other student is positioned.
  • Where roleplayers may enter.
  • Which areas should remain clear.
  • What could cause the situation to become unsafe.
  • When they would call a safety stop.

Stage 5 — Trigger Discipline

Explain that trigger discipline is a constant safety expectation.

Poor trigger discipline may demonstrate:

  • Lack of concentration.
  • Rushing.
  • Poor environmental awareness.
  • Failure to follow instructions.
  • Unsafe habits within the simulation.

Repeated concerns should result in the exercise being stopped and remedial coaching being delivered.

Trigger-Discipline Observation

During a stationary simulation, observe whether each student:

  • Remains attentive to trainer direction.
  • Avoids unnecessary trigger contact.
  • Maintains controlled behaviour.
  • Stops when corrected.
  • Applies the correction consistently.

This is an observation exercise, not a firing or weapon-operation drill.

Stage 6 — Positive Identification

Explain that students must separate:

Reported Information Information provided by another person or source.
Personal Observation Information personally seen or heard.
Confirmed Threat Information sufficiently supported within the scenario.
Assumption A belief that has not yet been confirmed.

Clothing, location, another officer's shout or a matching description should not automatically be treated as complete proof.

Scenario One — Conflicting Descriptions

Students are told that a subject in dark clothing may be armed. Two roleplayers in dark clothing are present, and no weapon is visible.

Ask:

  • What is known?
  • What remains unconfirmed?
  • What further information may be required?
  • Why would assumption be dangerous?
  • When should the exercise be paused?

Expected learning points

  • The description alone does not confirm identity.
  • Students should continue assessing the scene.
  • Clear communication is required.
  • Uncertainty should be acknowledged.
  • The trainer controls the scenario boundaries.

Stage 7 — Code44 Firearms Roles

SIG Sauer P226 Personal sidearm carried by trained and authorised ARU officers.
SIG MCX Primary operational carbine used during authorised deployments.
SIG 716 Specialist precision capability used during authorised roles.
Remington Shotgun Dedicated breaching platform, not a routine primary firearm.

Reinforce that practical use remains subject to trainer control and later authorised Academy instruction.

Remington Role Reminder

The Remington is a specialist breaching platform.

It must not be presented as the routine firearm for general patrol, ordinary building searches or standard engagements.

Detailed breaching instruction sits outside this module.

Stage 8 — Storage and Accountability

All firearms other than the officer's personal P226 sidearm must remain secured within the designated rear firearms-storage area of the ARV unless authorised for deployment.

This includes:

  • SIG MCX carbines.
  • SIG 716 precision rifles.
  • Remington breaching shotguns.

Students must understand:

  • Long firearms must remain secured when not deployed.
  • Every item must remain accounted for.
  • Unauthorised removal must be reported.
  • Returned equipment must be secured correctly.
  • Damage, loss or script issues must be reported.
  • Responsibility continues until the item is secured and returned.

Scenario Two — Equipment Script Failure

During training, a simulated firearm or animation stops behaving as expected because of a script issue.

Ask:

  • Should the student continue?
  • What should be communicated?
  • Who controls the restart?
  • Why should the issue not be ignored?

Expected learning points

  • The student should stop and inform the trainer.
  • The exercise should remain paused.
  • The trainer decides whether to reset or abandon the exercise.
  • Script uncertainty can affect the safety and fairness of the scenario.

Stage 9 — Supervised Safety Simulation

Run a basic simulation focused solely on:

  • Listening to commands.
  • Maintaining awareness.
  • Controlled movement.
  • Trigger discipline.
  • Clear communication.
  • Stopping when uncertain.

Do not introduce complex tactical objectives during this lesson.

One student should participate while the second observes. They then swap.

Peer Observation Task

The observing student should identify:

  • One thing completed safely.
  • One communication strength.
  • One point requiring improvement.
  • Whether trainer commands were followed.
  • Whether the student stopped appropriately when unsure.

Feedback must remain respectful and focused on behaviour.

Scenario Three — Missed Safety Command

During simulation, a trainer gives a stop command. One student continues moving because they are focused on the roleplay.

Ask:

  • What should happen immediately?
  • Why is continuing unacceptable?
  • Should the student automatically fail?
  • What remedial action may be appropriate?

Expected learning points

  • The exercise must stop.
  • The command must be acknowledged.
  • The trainer should identify why it was missed.
  • The command-response drill may need repeating.
  • Repeated disregard may affect progression.

Review Questions

  1. Who is responsible for safety during training?
  2. What should a student do when unsure?
  3. Why must trainer commands be followed immediately?
  4. What does safe-direction awareness involve?
  5. Why is trigger discipline important?
  6. What is positive identification?
  7. What should happen when a script fails?
  8. What is the role of the P226?
  9. What is the role of the MCX?
  10. What is the role of the SIG 716?
  11. What is the role of the Remington?
  12. Where must long firearms be stored when not deployed?
  13. What is the storage exception?
  14. When should a safety stop be called?

Expected Answers

  1. Everyone participating shares responsibility, with the trainer controlling the exercise.
  2. Stop and request clarification.
  3. They maintain control and prevent confusion or unsafe behaviour.
  4. Remaining aware of where the simulated firearm is directed and who is nearby.
  5. It prevents careless or unintended actions during simulation.
  6. Confirming the correct subject and relevant threat information rather than relying on assumptions.
  7. Stop, inform the trainer and await direction.
  8. Personal sidearm.
  9. Primary operational carbine.
  10. Specialist precision capability.
  11. Specialist breaching platform only.
  12. In the designated rear firearms-storage area of the ARV.
  13. The officer's personal P226 sidearm.
  14. Whenever safety, instructions, equipment or scenario conditions become unclear.

Instructor Observation Criteria

Command Response Responds immediately to trainer instructions.
Awareness Maintains awareness of officers and roleplayers.
Safe Direction Demonstrates controlled environmental awareness.
Trigger Discipline Avoids unnecessary trigger contact in simulation.
Positive Identification Separates confirmed information from assumption.
Communication Raises uncertainty promptly and clearly.
Accountability Reports mistakes or equipment issues honestly.
Professionalism Accepts correction and remains disciplined.

Immediate Safety Concerns

The exercise should be stopped immediately if a student:

  • Ignores a trainer safety command.
  • Continues while confused.
  • Displays repeated poor awareness.
  • Displays repeated trigger-discipline concerns.
  • Acts outside the exercise boundary.
  • Uses equipment contrary to trainer direction.
  • Attempts to turn the lesson into unsupervised training.
  • Behaves recklessly or unprofessionally.

Module Outcomes

Pass

The student consistently follows safety commands, communicates clearly and demonstrates satisfactory simulated safety behaviour.

Pass with Development Points

The student may progress but must address limited issues identified during observation.

Remedial

The student requires additional command practice, safety coaching or another supervised simulation.

Incomplete

Required attendance, simulation or assessment remains outstanding.

Unsuccessful

The student has not met the required safety standard after appropriate support and reassessment.

Common Student Errors

  • Focusing on the scenario instead of the trainer.
  • Continuing when unsure.
  • Responding slowly to a stop command.
  • Rushing to appear confident.
  • Failing to communicate confusion.
  • Treating reported information as confirmed fact.
  • Ignoring a script or equipment fault.
  • Becoming defensive when corrected.

Common Trainer Errors

  • Introducing complex tactics too early.
  • Teaching real-world weapon-operation procedures.
  • Failing to explain the simulation boundary.
  • Giving unclear safety commands.
  • Allowing one student to dominate.
  • Failing to stop repeated unsafe behaviour.
  • Assessing speed instead of discipline.
  • Failing to record safety-development points.

Closing Script

Suggested wording

“That concludes Module 5.

Firearms safety is not something you demonstrate once. It is a professional habit shown through discipline, awareness, communication and the willingness to stop when anything becomes unclear.

Module 6 will build upon these standards. The safety expectations from today remain in force throughout every later Academy exercise.”
