Trainer Module 1 — Welcome to ARU

Instructor Lesson Plan

ARU Academy • Cohort 001

Trainer-Only Resource

Lesson Overview

Module 1 introduces new students to the Armed Response Unit, the structure of the ARU Academy and the standards expected throughout training.

This session sets the tone for the rest of the course. Students should leave understanding that ARU membership is based upon professionalism, accountability, teamwork and continued development.

Lesson Module 1 — Welcome to ARU
Recommended Duration 60 minutes
Current Group Two new ARU students
Delivery Style Briefing, discussion and portal walkthrough
Assessment Observation and knowledge check
Pass Standard Satisfactory participation and 80% quiz score

Trainer Preparation

Before beginning the session, confirm:

  • Both students can access the Internal Database
  • Both students can open the ARU Academy
  • The Student Handbook is available
  • The Module 1 student page is working
  • The Module 1 knowledge check is ready
  • Student names, ranks and departments are confirmed
  • An attendance record is prepared
  • The current ARU command structure is accurate
  • All links within the Academy are working

Required Resources

  • Internal Database access
  • ARU Academy access
  • Student Handbook
  • Module 1 student page
  • Module 1 knowledge check
  • Discord voice channel or in-game classroom
  • Attendance record
  • Trainer assessment form

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the session, each student should be able to:

  • Explain the purpose of the Armed Response Unit.
  • Explain why the ARU Academy exists.
  • Identify the relevant ARU command structure.
  • Describe the expectations placed upon students.
  • Navigate the Student Area and Internal Database.
  • Explain how progression and qualification work.
  • Understand that attendance alone does not guarantee qualification.
  • Know who to contact for support.

Suggested Lesson Structure

Stage Time Trainer Action
Welcome and Introductions 10 minutes Welcome both students, introduce yourself and ask each student to explain their current role and reason for joining ARU.
What ARU Is 10 minutes Explain the purpose, responsibilities and culture of the unit.
Academy Structure 10 minutes Explain the modules, attendance expectations, assessments and progression process.
Standards and Expectations 10 minutes Cover professionalism, integrity, accountability, communication, teamwork and development.
Website Walkthrough 10 minutes Guide students through the Internal Database, Academy and Module 1.
Review and Knowledge Check 10 minutes Ask review questions, answer final queries and direct students to the Module 1 knowledge check.

Opening Script

Suggested wording

“Welcome to the Code44 Armed Response Unit Academy.

This course is designed to take you from being new to ARU through a structured programme of learning, practical development and assessment.

Today will explain how the Academy works, what the unit expects from you and what you must demonstrate before being considered qualified.

You are not expected to know everything on day one. You are expected to listen, ask questions, accept feedback and demonstrate improvement.”

Trainers do not need to read the script word-for-word, but the core message should remain consistent.

Stage 1 — Student Introductions

Ask each student to provide:

  • Name and rank
  • Current department or role
  • Previous relevant experience
  • Reason for joining ARU
  • One area they feel confident in
  • One area they want to develop
Trainer Note With only two students, encourage a proper discussion rather than rushing through introductions.

Stage 2 — Explaining the ARU

Explain that ARU provides specialist support during:

  • Incidents involving credible firearms threats
  • High-risk operations
  • Planned operations requiring authorised armed support
  • Incidents requiring specialist command or trained resources
  • Operations supporting frontline officers and other departments

Emphasise that:

  • ARU is not simply access to firearms or specialist vehicles.
  • Officers remain accountable for every decision.
  • Communication and judgement matter as much as technical ability.
  • ARU exists to support the wider police service.
  • Professional conduct applies during training and operations.

Discussion — What Makes a Good ARU Officer?

Ask both students to identify five qualities of a good ARU officer.

Strong answers may include:

  • Professionalism
  • Clear communication
  • Teamwork
  • Integrity
  • Accountability
  • Calm decision-making
  • Reliability
  • Willingness to learn
  • Ability to accept feedback
  • Respect for command structure
Trainer Prompt “Would you rather train someone with a good attitude and limited knowledge, or someone knowledgeable who refuses to listen? Why?”

Stage 3 — Explaining the Academy

Explain the current pathway:

  1. Student Handbook
  2. Module 1 — Welcome to ARU
  3. Module 2 — Professional Standards
  4. Module 3 — Legal and Decision-Making
  5. Module 4 — Equipment and Resources
  6. Module 5 — Communications
  7. Module 6 — Incident Management
  8. Module 7 — Teamwork and Leadership
  9. Module 8 — Final Assessment
  10. Graduation and qualification decision

Make clear that:

  • Modules should be completed in the directed order.
  • Attendance alone does not guarantee progression.
  • Students must participate and demonstrate improvement.
  • Assessment outcomes may include pass, development points, remedial or unsuccessful.
  • Questions are encouraged throughout the course.

Stage 4 — Student Expectations

Professionalism

Students must behave appropriately during lessons, deployments, Discord discussions and contact with the wider service.

Integrity

Students must be honest about mistakes, attendance, experience and assessment performance.

Accountability

Students are responsible for completing work, attending sessions and raising problems early.

Communication

Students should listen carefully, ask questions and provide clear information.

Teamwork

Both students should support one another. The course is not a competition.

Development

Students must accept feedback and demonstrate that they can apply it.

Two-Student Exercise — Cohort Standards

Ask both students to agree three standards they will hold each other to throughout the course.

Suggested areas:

  • Arriving prepared
  • Helping each other revise
  • Being honest about mistakes
  • Giving respectful feedback
  • Communicating when unavailable

Ask each student to identify one practical way they can support the other.

Stage 5 — Internal Database Walkthrough

Ask both students to locate:

  • The Internal Database homepage
  • The ARU Academy
  • The Student Area
  • The Student Handbook
  • Module 1
  • The SOP Library
  • The course timetable
  • The knowledge-check button

Allow students to navigate independently before offering assistance.

Scenario Discussion — Reporting a Mistake

During training, a student realises they gave incorrect information during an exercise. Nobody else appears to have noticed.

Ask:

  • What should the student do?
  • Why does honesty matter?
  • Should making a mistake automatically result in failure?
  • What would make the situation more serious?

Expected learning points

  • The student should report the mistake.
  • Mistakes can provide valuable learning.
  • Dishonesty is more serious than the original error.
  • The trainer should provide clear feedback and allow improvement.

Review Questions

Ask each student questions individually. Do not allow one student to answer everything.

  1. What is the purpose of the ARU Academy?
  2. Does attendance alone guarantee qualification?
  3. Who should you contact if you do not understand part of the course?
  4. Why is integrity important?
  5. What should you do if you cannot attend a session?
  6. Where are student learning materials stored?
  7. What should you do after receiving constructive feedback?
  8. What outcomes may follow an assessment?

Expected Answers

  1. To provide structured learning, development and assessment before operational qualification.
  2. No. Students must meet the relevant standards.
  3. An approved trainer, mentor or ARU supervisor.
  4. Integrity builds trust and ensures mistakes are reported honestly.
  5. Notify a trainer as early as possible and arrange catch-up learning.
  6. Within the Internal Database and ARU Academy Student Area.
  7. Listen, clarify, reflect and apply the feedback.
  8. Pass, pass with development points, remedial, incomplete or unsuccessful.

Instructor Observation Criteria

Engagement Participates appropriately.
Communication Speaks clearly and listens.
Attitude Demonstrates willingness to learn.
Professionalism Behaves respectfully.
Understanding Understands the Academy structure.
Teamwork Supports the other student.

Module Outcomes

Pass

The student participated appropriately, understood the Academy structure and completed the knowledge check.

Pass with Development Points

The student may progress but has specific development areas to address.

Remedial

The student requires further explanation, additional support or a repeat knowledge check before progression.

Incomplete

Required attendance, participation or assessment remains outstanding.

Unsuccessful

The student has not met the required standard after appropriate consideration.

Common Trainer Errors

  • Reading the student page word-for-word.
  • Allowing one student to dominate the session.
  • Failing to explain progression clearly.
  • Focusing too heavily on weapons rather than standards.
  • Rushing the website walkthrough.
  • Failing to record attendance or feedback.
  • Making qualification sound automatic.

Closing Script

Suggested wording

“That concludes Module 1.

You should now understand what the Academy expects, how the course is structured and where your learning materials are stored.

Your next module focuses on professional standards. Before then, complete the Module 1 knowledge check and raise any questions with the training team.

You are not expected to be perfect immediately. You are expected to be honest, professional and willing to improve.”

Trainer Completion Checklist

  • Both students attended
  • Introductions were completed
  • The purpose of ARU was explained
  • The Academy structure was explained
  • Standards and expectations were discussed
  • Both students completed the website walkthrough
  • Both students answered review questions
  • Individual feedback was recorded
  • Both students were directed to the Module 1 quiz
  • Module outcomes were recorded

Question Correct Answer / Expected Learning Marking Guidance

What is the main purpose of the Armed Response Unit? 		To provide specialist support during high-risk incidents and authorised operations. Award 1 point only for the correct multiple-choice answer.

What is the purpose of the ARU Academy? 		To provide structured learning, development and assessment before qualification. Award 1 point only for the correct multiple-choice answer.
Question 7
Does attending every Academy lesson automatically guarantee qualification? 		No. Students must meet the required knowledge, conduct and practical standards. Award 1 point for “No.”

Which qualities are expected of an ARU student?
  • Professionalism
  • Integrity
  • Accountability
  • Teamwork
  • Clear communication
  • Willingness to improve

  • Refusal to accept feedback
  • Personal recognition above team performance

Question 10
Where are approved student learning materials stored? 		Within the Internal Database and ARU Academy Student Area. Award 1 point for the correct multiple-choice answer.
Question 11
What should a student do after receiving constructive feedback? 		Listen, ask for clarification where required, reflect upon the feedback and apply it during future training. Award 1 point for the correct multiple-choice answer.
Question 12
What should you do if you make a mistake during training? 		Report it honestly, accept feedback and use the mistake as an opportunity to improve. Award 1 point for the correct multiple-choice answer.
Question 13
Who should you contact if you do not understand part of the course? 		An approved trainer, mentor or ARU supervisor. Award 1 point for the correct multiple-choice answer.
Question 14
Which information must not be shared outside authorised ARU channels?
  • Restricted SOPs
  • Assessment answers
  • Trainer-only lesson plans
  • Scenario information
  • Passwords
  • Personal student records
 Public recruitment information is not a restricted answer. Award 1 point only where all restricted items are selected and no incorrect option is selected.
Question 15
Explain why joining ARU carries additional responsibility. 		A satisfactory answer should recognise that:
  • ARU decisions may have serious consequences.
  • Officers must act professionally and responsibly.
  • Actions and decisions must be explained and justified.
  • Policy and command direction must be followed.
  • Specialist status does not remove accountability.
 Satisfactory: Clearly explains at least two relevant points.

Development required: Gives only a vague answer such as “because ARU is important” without explaining responsibility or accountability.
Question 16
Name three qualities that make someone a reliable ARU officer. 		Accept any three relevant qualities, including:
  • Professionalism
  • Integrity
  • Accountability
  • Teamwork
  • Clear communication
  • Reliability
  • Calm judgement
  • Willingness to learn
  • Ability to accept feedback
  • Respect for command structure
 Mark satisfactory where three appropriate qualities are provided. Ask for clarification if an answer is unclear or unrelated to professional ARU service.
Question 18
What is one area you personally want to improve during Cohort 001? 		There is no single correct answer. The response should identify a genuine and relevant development area, such as:
  • Communication
  • Confidence
  • Decision-making
  • APSTRA
  • Teamwork
  • Firearms knowledge
  • Vehicle familiarity
  • Accepting or applying feedback
 Do not score this as right or wrong. Record the response as an individual development objective and review it later in the Academy.

Knowledge Check Marking Standard

Questions 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 form the scored knowledge section.

  • Total automatically scored questions: 9
  • Recommended pass mark: 8 out of 9
  • Questions 15 and 16: Must also be judged satisfactory
  • Question 18: Record as a personal development objective

Pass

The student achieves at least 8 out of 9, provides satisfactory written responses and demonstrates appropriate participation during the lesson.

Remedial Review

The student scores 7 out of 9 or below, provides an insufficient written response or demonstrates a significant misunderstanding.

The trainer should review the incorrect areas with the student before allowing another attempt.
