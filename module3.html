Module 3 — Legal and Decision-Making

Code44 Police Service

Armed Response Unit Academy

Cohort 001

Module Overview

This module introduces the legal, policy and accountability principles that support decision-making within the Armed Response Unit.

Students are not expected to memorise every rule word-for-word. They are expected to understand the principles behind their decisions and explain why an action was necessary, reasonable and proportionate.

This module is written for Code44 roleplay. Current server rules, approved force policy and command direction always take priority.

Estimated Reading Time 30–40 minutes
Lesson Delivery Trainer-led discussion and tabletop exercises
Assessment Knowledge check and written decision exercise
Required Before Module 4

Important Policy Notice

This module supports Code44 Police Service roleplay and does not replace current force policy, server rules or command direction.

Where this page and an approved current policy differ, the current approved policy takes priority.

1. Learning Objectives

After completing Module 3, you should be able to:

  • Explain why operational decisions must be justified.
  • Describe necessity, proportionality and reasonableness.
  • Separate confirmed information from assumptions.
  • Consider reasonable alternatives before acting.
  • Explain the purpose of APSTRA.
  • Recognise when circumstances require reassessment.
  • Understand the importance of command updates.
  • Describe post-incident responsibilities.
  • Produce a clear and honest written rationale.

2. Responsibility Attached to Armed Policing

Armed policing carries an increased level of responsibility because decisions may have serious consequences for officers, subjects and members of the public.

Specialist status does not remove the need for restraint, communication, professional judgement or accountability.

An ARU officer should be prepared to explain:

  • What information was known at the time.
  • What threat or risk was identified.
  • What options were considered.
  • Why a chosen action was necessary.
  • Why the response was proportionate.
  • What changed during the incident.
  • What happened afterwards.

3. Core Legal and Policy Principles

Decisions within ARU should be based upon approved policy, current server rules and the following principles.

Necessity Was the action genuinely required, or was another reasonable option available?
Proportionality Was the response appropriate to the threat, risk and intended objective?
Reasonableness Did the decision make sense based on what the officer honestly knew at the time?
Accountability Can the officer clearly explain and record the decision?
Protection of Life Was preservation of life treated as the primary objective?
Fairness Were the rights and dignity of everyone involved considered?

4. Information Before Action

Good decisions depend upon reliable information. Officers should avoid treating an initial report as though every detail has already been confirmed.

Relevant information may include:

  • What has been reported.
  • Who provided the information.
  • Whether a weapon has been seen or confirmed.
  • The subject's description and location.
  • Behaviour displayed by the subject.
  • Presence of members of the public.
  • Presence of vulnerable persons.
  • Available resources and command support.
  • Whether the situation is changing.
Key Principle Clearly separate what has been reported, what has been confirmed and what remains unknown.

5. Five-Step Decision-Making Mindset

Students should use the following mindset when considering intervention, tactics or force.

  1. Identify the threat.
    What is happening, who is at risk and how immediate is the danger?
  2. Gather and assess information.
    Confirm what is known and identify what remains uncertain.
  3. Consider available options.
    Consider communication, containment, distance, time and support.
  4. Apply necessity and proportionality.
    Is the proposed response required and balanced against the threat?
  5. Communicate, record and review.
    Provide an APSTRA update and continue reassessing the situation.

6. APSTRA

APSTRA is the Code44 framework used to communicate and record the reasoning behind decisions, tactics and use of force.

A clear APSTRA update should identify:

Who Officer, rank, unit or callsign.
What Incident, threat or behaviour observed.
Where Clear location or area.
Risk Risk to officers, public, subject or vulnerable persons.
Action Intended action and concise justification.
Example “PC Bloggs, ARV-2. Reports of a firearm near Legion Square. One male matching the description, weapon not yet confirmed, high public presence. Establishing containment and requesting additional units.”

APSTRA updates should be concise, relevant and refreshed whenever the circumstances materially change.

7. Use-of-Force Considerations

Officers should not assume every incident must move through a fixed list of force options in a set order.

The appropriate response depends upon the actual threat, available time, subject behaviour, communication and risk to life.

Available options may include:

  • Clear communication and lawful directions.
  • Creating distance.
  • Containment and observation.
  • Using time to gather information.
  • Withdrawal or repositioning.
  • Requesting additional resources.
  • Using authorised force only where justified.

Force must stop or reduce when the justification for that force changes.

8. Reassessment and Changing Circumstances

Information can change quickly. A decision that was reasonable moments earlier may need to change when new information becomes available.

Officers should continually reassess:

  • Whether the subject matches the information provided.
  • Whether a weapon remains visible or confirmed.
  • Whether the threat is immediate.
  • Whether the subject is complying.
  • Whether members of the public are entering the area.
  • Whether the chosen action remains necessary.
  • Whether additional command direction is required.
Key Principle New information should be reflected in both the plan and the APSTRA update.

9. Command and Escalation

Students must understand when to request command direction and when an immediate life-saving decision may be required.

Command updates should include:

  • The current threat.
  • The location.
  • Subjects and persons at risk.
  • Actions already taken.
  • Resources present.
  • Resources required.
  • Any force used.
  • Any casualties or urgent medical needs.

Officers should not delay immediate action required to protect life solely because a commander is not yet present. Command should be updated as soon as reasonably possible.

10. Aftercare and Post-Incident Responsibilities

Responsibility does not end when the immediate threat has been controlled.

Post-incident priorities may include:

  • Confirming the immediate threat has ended.
  • Providing or requesting urgent medical assistance.
  • Protecting officers, subjects and the public.
  • Updating Force Control and firearms command.
  • Preserving the scene where possible.
  • Identifying witnesses.
  • Completing required reports.
  • Participating in debrief and welfare processes.
Core Principle Life-saving treatment takes priority over evidence preservation.

11. Reporting and Accountability

Reports should explain the decision honestly and in the order events occurred.

A clear written account should cover:

  • The information initially received.
  • What the officer personally saw or heard.
  • The threat and risk identified.
  • Options considered.
  • Action taken and why.
  • Any change in circumstances.
  • Any force used.
  • Aftercare and notifications completed.

Students should not write what they believe a trainer wants to hear. Accounts should reflect the officer's genuine observations and reasoning.

12. Revision Questions

Consider these questions before attending the trainer-led Module 3 lesson:

  1. What is the difference between reported information and confirmed information?
  2. What does necessity mean?
  3. What does proportionality mean?
  4. Why must officers continue reassessing an incident?
  5. What information should an APSTRA update contain?
  6. Why is use of force not an automatic ladder?
  7. When should command be updated?
  8. Why must force reduce when the threat reduces?
  9. What are the main post-incident responsibilities?
  10. What should a clear written account explain?
