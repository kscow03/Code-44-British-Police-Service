Module 4 — Equipment, Vehicles and PPE

Code44 Police Service

Armed Response Unit Academy

Cohort 001

Module Overview

Module 4 introduces students to the equipment, vehicles and personal protective equipment used by the Code44 Armed Response Unit.

This module focuses upon familiarisation, safe storage, accountability and the correct role of each capability. It does not teach practical firearms handling, live-fire skills or breaching procedures.

Students must understand that specialist equipment is issued for a defined purpose and remains the responsibility of the officer using it.

Estimated Reading Time 35–45 minutes
Lesson Delivery Trainer-led equipment and vehicle walkthrough
Assessment Equipment identification and knowledge check
Required Before Module 5 — Firearms Safety and Handling

Important Training Notice

This module is written for Code44 FiveM roleplay. Students must follow current server rules, approved ARU policy and trainer direction.

Students must not deploy, handle or remove specialist equipment unless authorised to do so by an approved trainer or appropriate commander.

1. Learning Objectives

After completing Module 4, you should be able to:

  • Identify the principal equipment used by Code44 ARU.
  • Explain the purpose of standard personal protective equipment.
  • Describe the roles of the SIG P226, SIG MCX and SIG 716.
  • Explain the specialist role of the Remington breaching shotgun.
  • Identify the marked and unmarked BMW X7.
  • Explain how firearms are stored within ARU vehicles.
  • Understand the exception for an officer's personal sidearm.
  • Describe pre-deployment and end-of-tour equipment checks.
  • Explain how faults, damage or missing equipment should be reported.
  • Understand personal responsibility for issued equipment.

2. Equipment Responsibility and Accountability

Specialist equipment must be treated as an operational responsibility, not as an entitlement or status symbol.

Officers are responsible for ensuring that equipment issued to them is:

  • Appropriate for the authorised task.
  • Present and accounted for.
  • Stored securely when not deployed.
  • Used only by trained and authorised personnel.
  • Returned to the correct location after use.
  • Checked for faults or damage.
  • Reported immediately if missing or defective.
Core Principle If you take responsibility for an item, you remain responsible for ensuring it is secure, accounted for and returned correctly.

3. Personal Protective Equipment

Personal protective equipment supports officer safety and operational effectiveness. PPE must be worn or carried as directed by the trainer, operational commander or current ARU policy.

Plate Carrier Worn to carry ballistic protection and authorised operational equipment. Personal Equipment
Ballistic Plates Protective inserts worn within the plate carrier. Personal Equipment
Ballistic Helmet Protective head equipment used during authorised deployments and training. Personal Equipment
Eye Protection Protective equipment used to reduce risk to the eyes during training and operational activity. Personal Equipment
Ear Protection Protective equipment used during firearms training and other high-noise activity. Personal Equipment
Gas Mask Specialist protective equipment used when the circumstances and authorised plan require it. Specialist Equipment

4. Helmet, Eye Protection and Ear Protection

Ballistic Helmet

The ballistic helmet forms part of the officer's protective equipment. It may also support authorised attachments such as eye protection, hearing protection or night-vision equipment.

Students should understand that the helmet must:

  • Be worn correctly.
  • Be checked before use.
  • Remain securely fitted.
  • Be stored carefully when not required.
  • Be reported if damaged or defective.

Eye Protection

Eye protection should be used during relevant firearms training, simulated exercises and other activity where directed.

Ear Protection

Ear protection should be used during firearms training and other high-noise activity. Students must follow the trainer's instructions throughout all range or simulated-firearms sessions.

5. Specialist Protective Equipment

Night-Vision Equipment Code44 ARU has access to night-vision equipment for authorised operations and training scenarios. Students must not deploy or operate night-vision equipment without trainer or command direction.
Gas Mask The gas mask provides a specialist protective capability where authorised by the tactical plan. Students should understand its purpose, storage location and accountability requirements.
Ballistic Shield The ballistic shield is a specialist protective resource used only by trained officers when authorised and appropriate. Detailed shield tactics are not covered within this module.

6. Medical Capability

Code44 ARU roleplays a comprehensive trauma and emergency medical capability. Medical equipment may be carried personally or stored within the ARV.

The available capability includes:

  • Individual first-aid equipment.
  • Tourniquets.
  • Chest seals.
  • Haemostatic dressings.
  • Pressure dressings.
  • Trauma shears.
  • Airway equipment.
  • Emergency blankets.
  • Oxygen equipment.
  • AED.
  • Medical backpack or trauma bag.

Tactical medical procedures will be covered in a later Academy module. Module 4 requires students only to understand what equipment is available, where it is stored and how it is accounted for.

Core Principle Medical equipment must be checked, available and replaced after use. Missing or used items must be reported before the vehicle returns to service.

7. Firearms Familiarisation

This section introduces the role of each firearm within Code44 ARU. Practical handling and firearms drills are covered in later modules.

Capability Role Storage and Responsibility
SIG Sauer P226
Personal Sidearm 		The P226 is the personal sidearm carried by trained and authorised ARU officers. The sidearm is the exception to the general requirement for firearms to remain secured within the rear vehicle storage.
SIG MCX
Primary Carbine 		The SIG MCX is the primary operational carbine used by trained ARU officers during authorised deployments. It must remain secured within the designated rear vehicle firearms storage unless it has been authorised for deployment.
SIG 716
Precision Capability 		The SIG 716 provides a specialist precision-rifle capability for authorised roles and operations. It must remain securely stored within the vehicle until authorised. Students should not assume that every ARU officer will automatically deploy with it.
Remington Shotgun
Breaching Platform 		The Remington is used as a specialist breaching tool. It is not the routine primary firearm for general ARU deployments. It must remain securely stored within the vehicle unless authorised for a breaching role. Detailed breaching procedures are covered separately and only for trained personnel.

Firearms Familiarisation Boundary

This module does not provide practical instruction in operating, loading, firing or deploying any firearm.

Students must wait until the relevant trainer-led firearms module before completing any practical handling or simulated-firearms activity.

8. Less-Lethal and Supporting Equipment

Taser Available to appropriately trained and authorised officers. It must be carried and used in accordance with current Code44 policy and server rules.
PAVA Available as an authorised officer-safety option. Students must understand that carrying PAVA does not remove the need for communication, judgement and accountability.
Flashbangs Code44 ARU has access to flashbangs where supported by the server script. They are a specialist capability and must only be used when authorised and supported by current server mechanics.

Code44 ARU does not currently have a beanbag shotgun capability.

9. ARU Vehicles

Code44 ARU currently operates two BMW X7 variants.

Marked BMW X7 Used as a visible armed-response vehicle and identifiable ARU resource. The vehicle carries secured firearms, medical equipment, PPE and other authorised operational resources.
Unmarked BMW X7 Provides an unmarked ARU capability for authorised deployments and operational requirements. The same security, accountability and equipment-check standards apply.

Crewing

ARV crews are normally double-crewed by trained ARU officers.

Both officers remain responsible for understanding the vehicle's equipment, confirming that firearms are secured and reporting faults or missing items.

10. Firearms Storage Within ARU Vehicles

Firearms other than the officer's personal sidearm must remain secured within the rear firearms storage of the vehicle when not deployed.

This includes:

  • SIG MCX carbines.
  • SIG 716 precision rifles.
  • Remington breaching shotguns.

The SIG P226 personal sidearm is the exception and may remain carried by the trained and authorised officer.

Students must understand that:

  • Vehicle firearms must not be left unsecured.
  • Firearms must remain in the designated storage until authorised.
  • Every firearm must be accounted for before and after deployment.
  • Unauthorised access must be reported immediately.
  • A firearm returned to the vehicle must be secured correctly.
  • The vehicle must not be left unattended with insecure firearms.
Key Rule Every firearm other than the officer's personal sidearm remains secured in the rear of the ARV unless deployment has been authorised.

11. Pre-Deployment and End-of-Tour Checks

Pre-Deployment Check

Before an ARV is placed into service, the crew should confirm:

  • The vehicle is present and serviceable
  • The marked or unmarked configuration is understood
  • All required firearms are present
  • All rear firearms are securely stored
  • Personal sidearms are accounted for
  • Required PPE is available
  • The medical kit is present
  • The ballistic shield is available where required
  • Night-vision equipment is present where authorised
  • Communications equipment is working
  • Any faults or shortages have been reported

End-of-Tour Check

Before the vehicle is stood down, the crew should confirm:

  • All deployed firearms have been returned
  • All rear firearms are secured
  • No equipment has been left at an incident or training location
  • Used medical items have been identified
  • Damaged equipment has been reported
  • Missing equipment has been reported
  • The vehicle has been checked for damage
  • The vehicle is ready for the next crew
  • Relevant logs or reports have been completed

12. Reporting Faults, Damage or Missing Equipment

Students must report any fault, damage or missing equipment as soon as it is identified.

A report should explain:

  • What item is affected.
  • Where the item should be located.
  • When the issue was discovered.
  • Whether the equipment was recently used.
  • Whether the vehicle can remain operational.
  • Who has been informed.
  • What immediate action has been taken.
Example During the pre-deployment check, the crew discovers that an item of medical equipment is missing. The vehicle should not simply be deployed without mentioning it. The shortage must be reported and resolved or formally accepted by the appropriate supervisor.

Hiding a fault or missing item is a professional-standards issue and may place other officers at risk.
